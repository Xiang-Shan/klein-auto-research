The experiment journey

Both tracks are registered: every row of the ledger is a pre-declared cell that was either measured or crashed. There is no incumbent and no keep, so the “frontier” for each track is simply its cells in the order they ran. Development cells and sealed confirmation cells are labelled separately below; the table that follows is generated from the ledger.

Track cot_gain: three development cells and one sealed cell

E0001 crashed. The first anchor run hit an HTTP rate-limit response after almost all of its calls had been made and pinned. That is an infrastructure fault (eight concurrent workers on an account shared with another study), not a bad idea, so the lead lowered the worker count and re-ran. E0002 answered all but one of its requests from E0001’s pinned shard and made one new call: this is how the public anchor was measured. Its paired gain was +0.01 with interval [-0.01, 0.03], against a registered rule value of 0.10 (P2 refuted), and its direct score of 0.95 was far above the 0.55 to 0.85 range the card had expected (P1 refuted).

E0004 repeated the paired comparison on 200 development twins and found the direct arm ahead by 0.025 (cluster interval [-0.065, 0.005]); P4 refuted. The experimenter’s decision lines were written before the next cell ran. The sealed cell E0005 was opened once, after a mandatory offline dry run that spent nothing: the gain was 0.000 with interval [-0.055, 0.045] (P5 refuted).

Track contamination: one development cell and one sealed cell

E0003 compared CoT on the 50 originals with their 200 development twins, template by template: gap 0.02, interval over templates [-0.015, 0.06] (P3 supported by its point-estimate rule, with an upper end above the 0.05 bound). The sealed cell E0006 gave a gap of 0.01, interval [-0.04, 0.065] (P6 supported by the same rule). The two sealed cells share ledger entries. E0006 made no new model call: it re-scored the 50 original answers E0003 had recorded and the 200 sealed CoT twin answers E0005 had recorded. Their agreement is by construction, so the sealed contamination cell is a second scoring of answers already in the ledger, not a second query of the model.

Replications, and the replay that is not one

Two salted re-queries re-asked the model: one of the public anchor (E0002) and one of the development contamination cell (E0003). Each re-query reproduced within its pre-registered tolerance (0.03 and 0.05), and each moved: the anchor gain went from +0.01 to -0.0067, and the gap’s sign flipped from 0.02 to -0.025 (a shift of 0.045). That is what temperature 0 on a hosted model looks like. An offline replay of E0002 also ran. It reproduced with a difference of 0, because it re-scored the same recorded answers. A replay is never a replication: it audits parsing, scoring and aggregation, and says nothing about the model.

The decision lines

Every refuted prediction received a dated decision line in the lab notebook before the next cell ran. Two decisions shaped everything after them. When the experimenter saw, from E0001’s pinned shard and before any score existed, that the direct arm did not comply with its instruction, the lead declined to change the prompt, so the instrument would not adapt to observed outputs. And after the first referee round the synthesist added two post-hoc controls and a family-wise interval check, labelled as such; they appear in the next section.
