The method taught

The method is a paired item comparison with an honest unit of uncertainty, plus a fresh-twin probe for contamination. Both are cheap, and both are easy to get subtly wrong.

Two arms, one scorer, every call pinned

Each item is sent twice to the same model at temperature 0, once under a direct instruction and once under a cot instruction. A fixed scorer extracts the integer the reply commits to (the number after the last Answer: marker, else the last number in the reply) and scores 1 if it equals the gold answer, else 0. The prompts, decoding parameters and scorer live in a library file the experiment cannot edit, so a cell chooses which items to measure and never how they are asked or graded. Every request and response goes through a call ledger that pins the exact request, the response, the model id actually served, and the token usage; the study therefore re-verifies offline.

That is why a pair of arms at 0.95 and 0.96 can be compared far more sharply than two marginal means, and also why, near a ceiling, the whole comparison rests on a handful of items. The contamination probe: originals against fresh twins

GSM8K is presumed contaminated. A twin is the same question text with new numbers, generated from a template by a procedure that regenerates the original byte-for-byte and whose answers an exact solver validates. If the model had memorized an original’s answer, its score on the original should exceed its score on the twins of that template. The comparison is paired by template, so a hard template counts against both sides of its own difference and the template mix cannot create a gap. Two honest limits come with the design. First, a twin is a fresh string, not a fresh kind of problem, so the probe sees instance memorization only. Registering rules, and what a point-estimate rule can decide

The registered predictions read the printed point estimate, not the interval. A rule at a threshold the truth sits exactly on is a coin flip, and a rule can be satisfied while its interval still reaches past the bound. The study keeps those two things apart: the notary adjudicates the rule as written, and the interval is the evidence for what the data can and cannot exclude.
