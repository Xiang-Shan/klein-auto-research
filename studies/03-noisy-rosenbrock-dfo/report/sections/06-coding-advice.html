Coding Advice

  1. Measure the floor before judging any delta [C4]. Five reruns of the identical config across seed blocks cost seconds and re-priced every later comparison: the bar became 0.5695, and the development block turned out to be the luckiest of five. A delta below your floor is weather, not climate.
  2. Run the dumb baseline first [C5]. Random search at the same budget would have re-framed this study before it began — and the clean-room audit's chance check is exactly where it surfaced. Budget one ledger row for it next time.
  3. Scale gain sequences from YOUR landscape, not from the paper's example [C6]. Apply Spall 1998's first-step rule to your measured gradient magnitudes; here it prescribes , four orders below the "textbook" 0.1 that diverged.
  4. Register crash predictions on mechanisms [C7]. "It will diverge" and "it will be non-finite" are different claims; only the denominator is structurally guaranteed to die. The ledger now teaches both.
  5. Let the harness keep your losers. E0004's 1e196 and E0005's ZeroDivisionError are this study's most-cited rows — and both resolve to their exact candidate commits because the loop commits before it runs.
