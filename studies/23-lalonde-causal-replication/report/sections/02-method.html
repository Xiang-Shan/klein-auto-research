You already know the core idea

If you have trained a domain classifier to tell target rows from source rows and then reweighted the source rows to look like the target, you have done the core of propensity weighting for the effect on the treated. The target population is the 185 men who took the programme. The source is 2,490 survey men (PSID-1) or 15,992 (CPS-1) who did not. What we cannot observe is what the trainees would have earned without training; the survey men, reweighted or matched to look like the trainees, stand in for it.

Selection on observables cannot be checked from the data; the benchmark checks it from outside. Overlap can be checked, and the study pins two diagnostics for it with every estimate: the largest single weight share, and the number of comparison men used. What the interval is, and is not

Every att cell reports a percentile bootstrap of 500 draws that resamples trainees and comparison men separately and re-fits the logit in each draw, but holds the 260 experimental controls fixed. So it is a range for the ATT estimate with the benchmark held fixed, not a range for the gap. For matching, the standard bootstrap is not valid at all (Abadie and Imbens), so that range is descriptive only. The registered rules read only point gaps, so no verdict depends on an interval.
