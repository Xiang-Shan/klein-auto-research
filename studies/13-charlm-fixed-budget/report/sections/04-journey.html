The Journey

Phase 0 — measuring the floor before comparing anything

Five identically-configured runs of the anchor recipe, differing only in seed, give a level: mean 1.56915 nats, standard deviation 0.00802952, range 0.021485. That spread describes how much a single FIT moves — it is provenance, never the bar. The bar a candidate must actually clear is the spread of a comparison: the ten unordered pairs among those same five checkpoints, each pair re-scored on identical validation windows and differenced. That paired spread has standard deviation 0.00747623 and range 0.026494, and the bar is nats — set from the comparison quantity, not the level quantity, because a keep IS a comparison.

The anchor

E0001 reruns the exact committed anchor recipe at a sixth seed the floor sweep never used, and scores 1.572174 nats — 0.3766 fit-noise standard deviations from the five-seed mean, comfortably inside the registered bound of two. P1 is supported: the recipe reproduces, and the floor is usable as registered. Two more checks ride along for free. The trainer’s own reported loss and the checker’s independently recomputed loss agree to 0.0 nats, against a declared tolerance of 0.01 — the first of eight runs where that holds. And the same checkpoint, re-scored under KLEIN_DEVICE=cpu and KLEIN_DEVICE=mps, prints 1.572174 both times: a device change moves nothing at the printed resolution. A full re-execution in a detached worktree, same seed, same device, different process, lands 0.000962 nats away — 0.119808 fit-noise standard deviations, because MPS training is not bit-deterministic even when nothing else changes.

Four registered levers, four discards

One idea per transaction, all at the same 2000-step budget, all adjudicated by the notary on the verifier’s printed block — never by prose:

  • E0002, linear warmup on a constant learning rate — 1.584747 nats, floors. P2 refuted, and refuted on the wrong side of “no effect”: warmup did not merely fail to help, it cost more than a full floor. The steps spent ramping up to the target rate are steps not spent learning, and a fixed budget never gives them back.
  • E0003, weight tying — 1.739149 nats, floors, 21.1718 fit-noise standard deviations from the anchor. P3 refuted by an order of magnitude, in the losing direction — the single largest effect measured anywhere in this study. Tying removes 8320 of 824320 parameters, 1.009317 percent, which is why the method card expected it to be invisible; forcing one matrix to be both the input lookup and the output classifier, with no logit scale to reconcile the two roles’ very different norms, was not invisible at all.
  • E0004, dropout 0.1 — 1.639808 nats, floors. P4 supported — the card’s least safe prior, because enough passes over the train partition can make a model regularization-limited, and dropout would then pay. It did not: at this budget the binding constraint is optimization, not generalization, and making every step noisier is a straight loss.
  • E0005, width 128 → 256 — 1.576240 nats, floors, on a model with 3.908075 times the anchor’s parameters. P5 refuted — inside the floor, in the wrong direction. A wider model gets the same number of gradient updates as a narrower one at a fixed STEP budget, and simply has more parameters to move with each of them; this is not the compute-optimal regime scaling-law doctrine describes, and the honest statement is that capacity was not the binding constraint here.

Four levers, four failures to clear the floor, two of them costing multiple floors. That pattern is itself the result that chose the next candidate.

The keep nobody registered

With every registered lever spent, the phase’s free slot went to the reading the first four runs raised: if the binding constraint is optimization progress per step, the cheapest direct test is a schedule change that touches nothing else. E0006, cosine decay from the anchor’s learning rate down to a tenth of it over the same 2000 steps, scores 1.519319 nats — floors better than the anchor, 0.052855 nats in absolute terms. Same parameters, same tokens, same arithmetic per step: only WHEN the learning rate is spent changed. It carried no registered prediction and is the only frontier improvement in the study.

A referee note on this exact figure: the first render distinguished the two curves by colour alone (same marker, same solid line). Fixed for the version above — the anchor now draws solid with a circle marker, cosine decay dashed with a square marker — so the two stay legible without relying on hue at all. Every value the chart carries was already independently legible from its text annotations, so nothing about the finding changed; the figure is simply easier to read now.

Does warmup’s verdict survive its partner?

A fair objection to E0002: warmup is almost always paired with decay in practice, because a high peak learning rate is only safe if it is later spent down, so testing warmup against a CONSTANT rate may have been an unfair trial. E0007 adds 200 steps of warmup on top of the new E0006 incumbent and scores 1.520867 nats — floors from the anchor but only 0.001548 nats, 0.103528 floors, from the E0006 incumbent it started from: inside the floor, neither cost nor benefit. P2’s registered verdict stands exactly as adjudicated against the anchor, but the honest advice sharpens: decay, and warmup is then optional — not “warmup is harmful.”

Six candidate transactions, six single ideas, six notarized verdicts: zero crashes, one keep. The searcher-versus-checker gap stayed at 0.0 nats on every one of them, and the steps/eval_context guardrails held at 2000 and 128 on every run — no candidate bought anything with extra compute.

The sealed look

The law requires a dry run before any real sealed access, spending nothing: klein run-one --final-test --dry-run executed the frozen E0006 configuration against development rows and printed confirmation before a single sealed character was ever read. E0008 then retrained the selected cosine-decay recipe on train + development — 111616 more characters than the development incumbent ever saw — and scored it once, on the sealed final tenth: 1.56628 nats, 2.259664 bits per character. That is 5.8485 fit-noise standard deviations from the development incumbent, well outside the registered band of two. P6 is refuted. The sealed run had MORE training data than the incumbent it is compared against, so this is not overfitting; it is 3.140679 measured floors of distribution shift between a contiguous final tenth of a concatenation of plays — different plays, different speakers, different proper nouns — and the rest of the corpus. A contiguous-block split buys leakage safety (the largest cross-partition 8-gram similarity anywhere is 0.030059) and pays for it with exactly this kind of shift, which a shuffled split would have hidden instead of measured.

One track, one sealed access: that arithmetic confirms the selected recipe’s LEVEL on unseen text and nothing else. The 0.052855-nat improvement of cosine decay over the anchor was measured entirely on the development partition, and no sealed measurement of the anchor exists to difference against — so the improvement itself stays exploratory by construction, while the sealed level, 1.56628 nats, is confirmed.
