Data

Everyone's first dataset is treated as innocent. It is not. Before any model ran, this study re-audited all 150 printed flowers from the committed bytes — not from citation, from the files in the repository — and the audit is the reason several later sentences are phrased the way they are.

The measurements are a lattice, not a continuum. All 600 measurement values sit exactly on the 0.1 cm grid. Petal width takes only 22 distinct values across 150 flowers. Calling these features continuous is a modelling convenience; the instrument was a ruler read to a tenth of a centimetre, and every claim about duplicates, near-duplicates and resolution downstream inherits that fact.

One exact duplicate, and it is famous. In the full 150 there is exactly one duplicated row-content group: iris rows 102 and 143, both virginica, (5.8, 2.7, 5.1, 1.9). Inside the hard pair it is the same single pair. The duplication is original to Fisher's own 1936 Table I — printed twice there — and the study carries the scope qualifier verbatim wherever it says so: "in the forensic sources we checked." Never "nobody ever noticed." At 0.1 cm resolution, identical measurements cannot be distinguished from two genuinely identical flowers, so the rows are never deleted. They share one group id and travel together through the outer split, all twenty metrology redraws, every arena fold and rung subset, and every inner cross-validation.

Five near-duplicates, ruled the other way. Five pairs in the full 150 are identical in three features and exactly one 0.1 cm step apart in the fourth — one of them inside the hard pair. The twin ruling is deliberately not extended to them: they differ in a recorded measurement, so they are distinct flowers, and saying so is a registered ruling rather than a judgement call made later.

The provenance diff, re-verified. scikit-learn ships the corrected copy of the table; the classic UCI file differs from it in exactly 3 cells, on two rows, both setosa. That was recomputed here from study 07's committed read-only UCI bytes rather than inherited as a citation — and the point of recomputing it is that the errata cannot touch a single hard-pair row.

Why setosa is dropped, in one number. Setosa's petal length stops 1.1 cm short of where the other two species begin: a clean one-feature gap. With setosa in frame, every method looks perfect and no method question can be asked. The three-class problem and the two-species hard pair answer genuinely different questions, and this study registers only the second — versicolor against virginica, the overlapping pair, 100 rows, with the full table's classes standing at exactly 50/50/50.

The split, and the consequence it forced. The declared split is group-aware 60/20/20 under seed 20260912, with no redraw permitted under any outcome. The twin group landed in TRAIN — which means a multi-row group sits in the non-sealed pool, and study 08's argument that a row-level inner cross-validation was harmless does not survive the move. Every internal split in this study is group-aware by construction because of where two flowers happened to fall. That is the useful shape of a data ruling: it is not a paragraph in a card, it is a constraint that changes the code.

The seed itself has a history worth one line. An earlier candidate seed was retired before any gate, because a pre-gate staging smoke had scored development and sealed Briers under it. The logs were deleted, the seed was retired on the precedent set in study 07, and the split was re-registered before the consult acknowledgement — disclosed in the scouting ledger rather than quietly reused.
