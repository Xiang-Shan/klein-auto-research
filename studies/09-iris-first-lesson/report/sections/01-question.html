The Question

Ninety years after Fisher fitted a discriminant to these flowers, two studies in this repository have asked the obvious modern question — can anything from the last nine decades earn a keep-sized win over the 1936 rule on his own hard pair? — and both came back with no keep earned. Study 09 asks a different question, and it asks it first.

Can the comparison be won at all? Every one of those contests was decided against a resolution threshold: a challenger must improve by at least as much as the measurement's own noise. Brier scores cannot go below zero, so the largest gain arithmetically available to anyone is the incumbent's own score. When that score is smaller than the threshold, the contest is finished before the first fit — not lost, unwinnable. Study 08 worked that ratio out by hand, in a comment, after the fact. Study 09 registers it as the lead result and publishes it before any challenger number exists. That reordering — measurement permission, then model comparison — is the whole study.

The first lesson of data science, re-asked as six registered questions:

  1. RQ0 — claim permission. For each frozen challenger, is the Brier comparison arithmetically capable of clearing that challenger's own paired resolution threshold? And at the ledger's single-scalar scale, is a keep possible for anybody?
  2. RQ1 — the contest. At nominal rung 60, does any challenger clear both its candidate-specific bar and the registered selection guard, at an open rung?
  3. RQ2 — sample size. As training rows fall from 60 to 8, how do predictive risk and resampling instability move — and does shrinkage LDA lead plain LDA descriptively where covariance estimation is fragile?
  4. RQ3 — feature sufficiency. How much probability quality does petal-only LDA retain, and does the sepal-only positive control fire at every measurable rung?
  5. RQ4 — metric meaning. Where ranking metrics saturate, do the probability metrics still order the room?
  6. RQ5 — the simulation lane. Under four known data-generating processes, how do squared probability bias, training-set variance and irreducible uncertainty trade as n grows?

The lineage, declared before anything was measured. This is the third study on the same one hundred flowers. Study 07 published every row's values in its public ledger and sidecars; study 08 additionally published both of its sealed values and the full row-id lists of every arena partition. Nothing about these flowers is unseen. The rows behind this study's seal are procedurally fresh only — the registered protocol never conditions selection on them, but nobody is blind to them, and the words blind, independent replication, untouched and virgin data are banned from every artifact this study produces. Seeds, per-candidate bars, the guard family, the branch rules, the coda bands and the finalize paths were all locked before the first 09 measurement. That lock, not blindness, is the integrity claim.

One word to disarm in advance. The study completes carrying klein's exploratory label. That is a protocol-completion label, pre-registered as the Branch-B outcome before any run — it records which sealed path the record selected, not how good the evidence is, and it never translates into scientific language.
