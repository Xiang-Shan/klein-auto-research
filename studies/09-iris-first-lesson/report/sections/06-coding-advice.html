Coding Advice

Below is the study's entire mutable surface. In a klein v2 study, train.py is the only file a candidate transaction edits, and it edits exactly one line of it — FAMILY. Everything else is the registry, the split, and the registered auxiliary block. Read it as an argument about where the knobs should not be.



Read the bytes above with erratum E1 in hand: line 78 hardcodes
seed=20260909, the retired namespace — the exact defect class
friction #5 asks the framework to make impossible (an evaluator that can
silently split differently from the fingerprinted contract). We publish the
file as committed, defect and all; that is what a ledger is for.



    


  1. Publish the permission map before the tournament. One table —
each candidate's headroom against its own floor — told this study, before any
challenger ran, that 3 of 7 comparisons could not be won at their own resolution and that
no scalar-bar keep was available to anyone. That is not a footnote; it is what makes
"nobody displaced the anchor" a sentence worth printing rather than a shrug. Compute it,
commit it, and only then run the roster.
    2. 


  2. Name the estimand of every floor, out loud, in the contract. A
resolution threshold without a named estimand is not a decision rule, it is a number. This
study registered paired-comparison and published the marginal spread beside it
— and was rewarded with a falsified prediction: 2 of 7, where 5 of 7 had been
registered. If it had quoted only one floor type it would have been wrong and would never
have found out.
    3. 


  3. Keep the two headroom numerators apart by force. The ledger's
numerator is a declared-split score; the per-candidate numerator is a metrology mean over
redraws. They sit close enough together that prose will swap them. Ban the conflation in the
contract, and print each numerator's name in the sidecar column header, as this
study's headroom table does.
    4. 


  4. Controls prove the instrument; they are not padding. Two extra
registered families cost minutes and bought the strongest sentence in the findings: an
apparatus that closed every contest still separated the sepal-only control at all six rungs.
Without it, "nothing cleared the bar" is indistinguishable from "the ruler was
broken."
    5. 


  5. Pass group-aware split policies, never split indices. A
group-aware outer split proves nothing if a calibration wrapper or a hyper-parameter tuner
resplits rows internally. Study 08 could argue its way out because no multi-row group sat in
its pool; one different seed later, the twins landed in TRAIN and that argument evaporated.
Precomputed policies survive the move; absolute index lists are correct exactly once, on
the data they were computed from.
    6. 


  6. Two smoke laws, and the second one cost a seal. First: any subagent
smoke runs on synthetic frames only — never the real prepared file, never the
declared split. This study wrote that law after a pre-gate staging smoke forced it to retire
a split seed. Second, and this is the lesson E0011 paid for in full:
smoke the exact sealed configuration before you record the confirmation
acknowledgement. The crash was a manifest-key mismatch between two modules that had
each been integration-tested against their own mock manifest and never against the real one.
A one-line dry run of the real configuration would have caught it. The seal spent anyway,
because a seal that survives an unlucky outcome is not a seal.
    7. 


  7. Assert your seed domain in every file that generates seeds — including
derived ones. Both predecessors crashed a namespace on the same overflow. Derived
schemes are where it bites: a base plus an offset plus an index is easy to write and easy to
push out of range. Assert in the header, not in the postmortem.
    8. 


  8. Capture your tooling's stdout into committed logs. Verification and
the frozen analysis both print to a console nobody keeps. A capture wrapper turns "we
checked" into an artifact a reviewer can open — which is how the 19-check verification
and the guard's own seed disclosures became evidence rather than recollection.
    9. 


  9. Full precision in the lock; round only at presentation. The binding
floor in this study is 0.07959988633051922 and the scalar derived from it is 0.08. Both live
in the artifacts at full width; the rounding happens in a sentence, once, where a reader can
see it. A lock that stores the rounded value has quietly thrown away the only thing that
makes the derivation checkable.
    10. 





The pattern underneath all nine: none of the findings here transfer, and all of
the habits do. Nothing about a hundred irises tells you anything about your data.
Measuring the resolution before the contest, naming what each threshold is a threshold
of, and keeping the failures where a reviewer trips over them — that survives
the change of subject.
