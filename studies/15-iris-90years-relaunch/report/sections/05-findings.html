Findings & insights

Twelve notarized runs across three tracks asked whether ninety years of classification research separates versicolor from virginica better than Fisher's own 1936 discriminant. None of the four post-1936 challengers cleared the incumbent by any resolvable amount: on the development block three of them tied it exactly at 1.000000 and the fourth lost; in the one sealed access the selected challenger lost -0.012821 val_auc. But the study's most useful output is a failure of its own instrument — the primary metric pegged at its own ideal, and that single fact reshaped everything downstream of it.

Why the metric pegged, and why every instrument built on it inherited the saturation

ROC-AUC was chosen for resolution: on 25 development flowers accuracy moves in steps of 0.04, one whole flower, while AUC over 156 ordered pairs resolves to 0.0064. That reasoning is sound and still is — it simply missed that a finer ruler is worthless against a quantity that has already reached its bound. On this partition the 25 development flowers are perfectly rank-separated: every versicolor scores below 0.02, every virginica above 0.45. Once a sample is cleanly rank-separated, every resample of it with replacement is cleanly rank-separated too — that is arithmetic, not a coding artifact — so the percentile bootstrap returns ci_width=0, the paired bootstrap returns a delta of exactly 0 on all 1000 replicates, and the split lottery returns 0 spread over five redraws. minimum_delta measures to 0; every floor-normalized key stops printing; the headroom law returns no number at all. The sealed block reproduces the same peg (val_auc=1.000000 again, on 25 flowers nobody had looked at), so this is a property of the species pair under these four measurements at this sample size, not one lucky development draw.

4 printed keeps, 0 resolvable wins

With minimum_delta measured at zero, the frontier rule is satisfied by an exact tie, and four of five modern-track development cells filed as keep. A ledger reader who counted keep rows without reading delta_vs_reference would conclude that three of four post-1936 methods improved on Fisher. None did — delta_vs_reference is 0.0 or -0.0 on every single one. This is why prediction P9 (“zero of the four families earns a keep”) is recorded refuted on the letter of its own count while the science it was written to test — ninety years of research producing no measurable separation gain — is supported.

The fourth surprise: a calibration reversal

On development, the boosted tree was not merely tied on ranking — it was the parade's best-calibrated model by a wide margin, Brier 0.000945 and log-loss 0.012827 against Fisher's LDA at 0.017482 and 0.061537. On the sealed rows the same recipe collapses to Brier 0.118994 and log-loss 0.750798 — two orders of magnitude worse than its own development calibration, while its ranking only slipped by -0.012821 val_auc. The sealed LDA-family cell that scored the same rows (the petal-only comparison) sits at Brier 0.057828 and log-loss 0.164260 — a far milder shift.

Read this as the textbook signature of a high-capacity learner memorizing 74 rows: the boosted tree's confidence was fitted to the training partition even where its ordering generalized. This is a mechanism interpretation, not a registered claim — the study's next-steps section names the direct repair.

The ablation's own honest wrinkle

Sepal length and sepal width alone lose a real, large, correctly-directed amount of separation — -0.185897 val_auc on development, a wider -0.8547 measured floors on the sealed block — and still fail the pre-registered “at least one floor below” bar, twice, because the ablation track's own floor (0.28125) was measured from this exact contrast's own paired-bootstrap spread. A one-floor rule then demands roughly one and a half times the effect's own mean magnitude. “Refuted” here means the registered bar was not cleared, never that no difference was found — and both numbers belong in any honest account of this result.
