The question

In 1936, R. A. Fisher published a linear discriminant fitted on a hundred iris flowers and showed that a single straight-line combination of four measurements could separate two overlapping species. Ninety years of classification research followed — logistic regression, nearest-neighbour rules, kernel machines, gradient-boosted trees — each capable, in principle, of finding curvature a straight line cannot. This study puts the question as a decision an ordinary practitioner could act on:

On the exact species pair Fisher studied, does any post-1936 classifier separate versicolor from virginica measurably better than his own 1936 discriminant, on flowers neither method has seen? And is the separating signal really in all four measurements, or do the two petal measurements alone carry essentially all of it?

This is a deliberate, independent redo. An earlier study in this repository looked at the same pair of species; this one is typed, gated and run without opening a single file from it, so that whatever it finds is arrived at on its own evidence, not copied or avoided (scouting_ledger.md; research_plan.md's “Out of scope, deliberately”). Comparing the two studies is left to the reader.

The contract, registered before any evidence existed

A predict study, modality tabular, profile generic, over the four measurement columns of 99 iris flowers (sklearn:load_iris, restricted to versicolor and virginica). Three tracks, not one, because the headline question is a comparison and a track gets exactly one sealed access: a single-track study could confirm the incumbent's level but never the gap.

  • fisher (registered, estimate) measures the incumbent's own level, with a bootstrap interval.
  • modern (frontier, predict) holds Fisher's discriminant as the incumbent; the four post-1936 challengers — L2 logistic regression, 5-nearest-neighbours, an RBF-kernel support-vector machine, a histogram gradient-boosted tree — climb it one at a time.
  • ablation (registered, test) maps which measurements carry the signal: petal-only against sepal-only against all four.

Every track shares one primary metric, ROC-AUC (val_auc, higher is better) — chosen before any byte was read because it is threshold-free and resolves finer than accuracy on a 25-flower block. Sixteen predictions, P0 through P15, were registered with arithmetic rules before the first row was fitted, and every headline number in the sections that follow is a copy of a value pinned in claims.lock, results.tsv or aux_metrics.tsv.

Three things to take away, and none of them is “LDA still wins”

  • The primary metric saturated. Fisher's own discriminant scores a perfect ROC-AUC of 1.000000 on the development block and again on the sealed block. Every noise-floor recipe Phase 0 ran on the fisher and modern tracks came back with a spread of exactly zero, so the measured keep bar on both tracks is 0 — not left unmeasured, measured to be zero.
  • A zero floor turns a tie into a keep. With the bar at zero, the frontier rule primary_metric >= incumbent + minimum_delta is satisfied by an exact tie. Four of the five modern-track development cells filed as keep; zero of the four challengers resolvably beat Fisher's discriminant by any amount the contract could certify. In the one sealed comparison the study did make, the selected challenger lost.
  • The ablation asked a different question, and it is answerable. The two petal measurements alone reproduce the all-four score exactly, on both the development and the sealed partition. The two sepal measurements alone lose a real, large amount of separation — and still miss a pre-registered bar built from that same effect's own spread. Both halves of that surprise are load-bearing for what this tutorial teaches in section 4.
