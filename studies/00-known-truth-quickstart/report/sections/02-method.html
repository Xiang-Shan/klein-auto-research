The method

Two ideas do all the work here, and neither of them is a model.

The ceiling

Imagine grading weather forecasters. Even a forecaster who knows the true probability of rain for every single day will not score perfectly, because it sometimes fails to rain on an eighty-percent day. There is a best possible score, and the world's coin flips set it, not the forecaster. That is the Bayes ceiling.

Two warnings the field paid for, repeated here. First, means only “not arithmetically excluded” — never “worth trying”; the attainable ceiling usually sits well below the ideal one.
