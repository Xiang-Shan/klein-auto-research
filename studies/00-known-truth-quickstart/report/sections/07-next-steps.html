Next steps

  1. Give the replication path something it can copy. Either declare the truth as part of the prepared artifact, or recompute the ceiling from the generator's committed coefficients instead of reading a cache. Then re-run the replication and watch the record come back reproduced. Cheapest fix, highest value.
  2. Measure a paired floor. Every delta here is paired — each run refits its reference on the same rows — but it is divided by a marginal floor, because that is the bar a frontier keep must clear. A paired-bootstrap floor would be the sharper denominator, and the two side by side would show what the conservative choice costs in resolution.
  3. Vary the sample size and watch the headroom close. Both terms of the ratio move with the number of rows: more rows shrink the floor and let the boosted tree get closer. The same four rungs at several table sizes would turn a single reading of the detection limit into a curve.
  4. Try a middle rung. The permission to say anything about capacity here comes from one extreme point. A shrunk, early-stopped boosted tree between the two measured settings would say whether the loss is a cliff or a slope.
