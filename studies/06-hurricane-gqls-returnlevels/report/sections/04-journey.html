Two tracks, two frontiers

Each track's development frontier is a step function of keeps; discards sit off it as retained evidence; the purple star is that track's single sealed final-test access, drawn deliberately off the frontier because sealed evidence is excluded from adaptive selection.

adaptive-1 — an honest discard that asked the right question

Nothing may be compared before the ruler is calibrated. E0001 did two jobs: prove the pipeline is looking at the thesis's sample, and fit one single cell — gQLS lognormal at (0.05, 0.95) — against the published 22.79/0.82.

Both halves passed. The run was still discarded. The identity gate verified 12 quantities at max |dev| 3.53e-05; the single cell landed inside the 0.005 resolution (μ̂ 22.788710, σ̂ 0.824890, worst parameter deviation 0.004890). But the max_abs_w_deviation guardrail came in at 0.4940 against a limit of 0.10, and max_abs_p_deviation at 0.059404 against 0.02. The parameters matched and the goodness-of-fit statistic did not.

That is not a contradiction — it is a diagnostic. W is a quadratic form in the residuals, so it is far more sensitive to the exact position of the eight grid points than a two-parameter regression line through them is. A discard that fails on the sensitive quantity while passing on the insensitive one is telling you the grid itself is slightly wrong. The registered default convention was hazen — because that is what the data-identity gate demanded. The guardrail had just said it could not also be what the estimators use.

The same phase measured the floors: the numerical floor at 8.8e−17 (recording ≈0 is the finding), the k = 5 quantile-convention sweep at a spread of 0.018498 = 3.7× the resolution, and the decision track's paired log-return-level bootstrap SE at 3.461 — of which 3.59 comes from the log-Cauchy arm alone.

adaptive-2 — the convention, as a detective story

This is the part worth reading slowly, because it is a reproduction failure that turned into the study's most transferable finding.

The first clue: a table that fit too well. Before any modeling, the data card had noted that Table 6.8's descriptive quartiles reproduce only under Hazen plotting positions — MATLAB's quantile default — and under nothing else, by margins up to 0.62. A descriptive table that matches one convention exactly is not a coincidence; it silently identifies the software that produced it. So the study registered hazen as its default. That was the right call on the evidence available, and it was wrong.

The second clue: a guardrail breach that would not go away. E0001's W deviation of 0.4940 was the first warning. E0002 scaled the same convention to the full 18-cell grid and the problem scaled with it: mean 0.009303 — passing the mean prediction — but max_abs_param_deviation 0.031279, breaching the 0.02 guardrail outright, with max |ΔW| at 1.2855 and max |Δp| at 0.15813. Only 13 of 36 parameters were inside the resolution. The descriptive convention cannot reproduce the estimation tables. Recorded as a predicted discard, not a surprise — the prediction was written into the candidate description before the run.

The resolution: the thesis had already answered. Not in its results, in its definitions. Chapter 2's opening paragraph defines the empirical quantile as F̂⁻¹(p) = X_(⌈np⌉) — NumPy's inverted_cdf. The descriptive table was produced by one piece of software; the estimators were specified by the mathematics. Both statements are in the same chapter, and neither says the other exists.

E0003 changed one configuration line — CONVENTION = "inverted_cdf" — and the track's only KEEP landed:

The mechanism, stated so it generalizes. At n = 3,000 the nine NumPy quantile conventions agree to four decimals and nobody notices. At n = 30 they disagree in the second decimal — which is exactly the decimal a paper reporting 22.79 / 0.82 is claiming. The convention sweep measured the whole spread on the track metric: inverted_cdf 0.002754 · hazen 0.009303 · normal_unbiased 0.011024 · median_unbiased 0.012036 · weibull 0.021252. Only one of the five lands inside the resolution the paper prints at.

The one cell that missed — and how the max guardrail earned its keep

The single over-resolution parameter of 36 is the (0.10, 0.90) log-Gumbel μ̂. Table 6.9 prints 22.34; our fit gives 22.358687, a deviation of 0.018687 — inside the 0.02 guardrail, but 3.7× the resolution.

The discriminating evidence is internal to the thesis. Table 6.10's g3 column prints 22.36 for the same fit — 0.0013 away from ours. So the reproduction does not disagree with the thesis; it disagrees with one printing of the thesis and agrees with the other. The study's own ledger records the verdict as a named aux metric: gumbel_g3_typo_verdict = table_6_9_is_the_typo.

Why this is worth a paragraph. A replicator without a max-deviation guardrail would never have seen it. That cell moves the mean by 0.0005 — one tenth of the resolution — so it would have been averaged away without a trace. The guardrail that exists to “stop one broken cell hiding behind a good average” earned its keep on a cell that turned out not to be a bug at all. Both outcomes are wins: if it had been our bug, the guardrail catches it; because it was not, the guardrail produced a small, citable correction to the published record.

E0004 — the p-values that were too good to be a bootstrap

RQ2 asked a cost question: the thesis prescribes a B = 1000 parametric bootstrap for the W_out p-value, which is expensive; is a plain χ²₂₃ reference indistinguishable? E0004 implemented both honestly (r = 25, B = 1000, seed 20260731) and returned two measurements that pull in opposite directions.

  1. The bootstrap is not numerically redundant. Our honest bootstrap departs from the χ²₂₃ reference by mean 0.0814 and max 0.2869 across the 18 cells — far beyond the registered 0.02 tolerance. The registered prediction is falsified.
  2. And yet 16 of 18 published p-values sit within 0.005 of the χ² reference — tighter than a B = 1000 bootstrap's own Monte-Carlo standard error (≈0.016 near p = 0.5) can be. Agreement that tight is not something a bootstrap can produce.

The two divergent cells are (0.02, 0.98) log-Logistic, which prints 0.16 against a χ² reference of 0.5939 (our bootstrap: 0.534), and (0.10, 0.90) log-Laplace, printing 0.55 against 0.4496 (our bootstrap: 0.486).

Wording discipline, applied deliberately. This is an observation about the reproducibility of printed numbers and nothing more. The published W_out statistics reproduce essentially exactly — 20.7905 against a printed 20.79, 23.1927 against 23.19 — so the fits and the test statistic are reproduced. It is only the route from statistic to p-value that cannot be reconstructed from the text as prescribed. Section 7 turns it into a one-sentence question for the author rather than an erratum.

E0005 — the Σ★ falsifier, the study's hardest gate

A wrong Σ★ fails silently. It still produces a fit, still produces plausible parameters, and nothing in the arithmetic complains. The pre-registered falsifier was therefore a specific number, with a STOP attached: reproduce the log-Cauchy σ̂ split between ordinary and generalized QLS — published as 0.23 versus 0.49 — or declare the covariance mis-specified and halt the study.

Passed exactly. Whole-arm means against the Table 6.10 originals: o2 0.0019355, o3 0.0014666, g2 0.0025893, g3 0.0019513, MLE 0.0021359. The closed-form covariance built from Serfling's theorem — written down before any data was seen — reproduces a published 2.11× efficiency split on real data. That is the moment the implementation became trustworthy.

The same phase ran an off-ledger k-sensitivity measurement, recorded in program.md and flagged in the findings as descriptive evidence with no run manifest. It refuted both halves of its registered prediction: parameters move by 0.1223 across k ∈ {8, 10, 15, 25} and 0.1040 across six conventions — 24.5× and 20.8× the reporting resolution. k moves things more than the convention does, and neither is a nuisance. The thesis pins k = 8 with inverted_cdf; under that pin, reproduction is exact.

E0006 set the baseline and already falsified one registered prediction: leave-top-1-out alone moves the MLE 1-in-100 by −25.68% (55.522 → 41.263 $bn), not the predicted >40%. The >40% level is first reached at leave-top-3 (−44.56%) and by 5× contamination (+58.06%), so the run-level metric held at 58.06 while the specific prediction did not.

E0007 is the study's punchline, and it arrived as a discard. The registered prediction was that gQLS log-Cauchy would be under ⅓ of MLE's instability — below 19.35. It came in at 62.94: 3.25× the predicted ceiling, and 1.08× the MLE baseline it was supposed to beat. The worst of the four contenders. Section 5 takes the refutation apart in three parts, because any one of them alone misleads.

E0008 isolated the mechanism by changing exactly one thing. Same estimator, same trim, same data, same GoF gate — only the family changes, log-Cauchy to lognormal, which changes only the standard quantile at p = 0.99 from 31.82 to 2.326. Instability: 62.94 → 41.33, a 21.62 pp move = 21.6× the ordering delta.

E0009 widened the trim from (0.05, 0.95) to (0.10, 0.90), raising the breakdown point from 0.05 to 0.10 — from “one bad record in thirty” to “three.” Instability fell again to 27.58, and the leave-top-1 stress collapsed from 19.02% to 5.66%. And the fit got better: W p rose from 0.5153 to 0.7310, matching the published 0.73 at that trim. The registered prediction had budgeted a goodness-of-fit cost for the wider trim; the cost came out negative.

Sealed confirmation — labelled separately, as it must be

Two runs, one per track, each spending that track's single sealed access against pre-registered published truth. Neither is on the adaptive frontier; both are excluded from selection by construction, which is why both appear as discard in the ledger despite being the study's strongest evidence.

E0010 — the full Table 6.10 grid (reproduction)

Five estimator arms × six families × original and 10×-modified data = 60 cells, 120 parameters that adaptive work never touched.

  • Mean |Δθ| 0.002026, max 0.005195, 119/120 inside the reporting resolution — better than the adaptive frontier, on four times as many parameters.
  • Every QLS arm's parameters are unchanged by the contamination, exactly. o2*, o3* and g3* are byte-identical to their unstarred twins (arm means 0.0019355 / 0.0014666 / 0.0019513 in both columns); g2* differs from g2 only through the published print (0.0026218 vs 0.0025893).
  • MLE moves as published. MLE*/lognormal reproduces at μ̂ 22.876935 (printed 22.88) and σ̂ 1.097494 (printed 1.10) against the clean 22.800182 / 0.833868 — a +31.6% move in σ̂ from one corrupted record in thirty.
  • The single over-resolution cell of 120 is g2*/log-Gumbel at 0.005195, and it is a printing artefact: our σ̂ = 0.734805 does not move under contamination, and Table 6.10 prints that same unchanged estimate as 0.73 in one column and 0.74 in the next — a last-digit rounding boundary.
  • A pleasing bonus the method card predicted in advance: Pareto I's contaminated σ̂ moves by exactly log(10)/30 = 0.076753 (1.258900 → 1.335653), with μ̂ = 21.541282 unchanged because its MLE is the boundary estimator min(x).
