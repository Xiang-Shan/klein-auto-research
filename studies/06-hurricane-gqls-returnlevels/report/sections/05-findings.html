Findings

Start with the number a committee can act on.

Take one record out of thirty — the 1926 Great Miami hurricane at $72.303bn — and multiply it by ten, the way a decimal-point slip or a double-counted recovery does. On maximum likelihood with a lognormal, the fitted 1-in-100 event loss goes from $55.5bn to $110.7bn, +99.4%. On gQLS-lognormal at trim (0.10, 0.90), the same corruption produces $56.7bn → $56.7bn, 0.0% — bit-identical in μ̂, σ̂ and all three return levels.

Both halves of that pair are on-ledger and sealed. The 0.0% is E0011, the decision track's single sealed access, run against the thesis's exact modification. The +99.4% is formed from E0006's clean MLE fit (55.522182) and E0010's sealed MLE* parameters (μ̂ 22.876935, σ̂ 1.097494), which give 110.700 — derived on-ledger, never re-fitted for the page.

The RQ5 punchline, in three parts that must be quoted together

gQLS log-Cauchy is the family the thesis's own goodness-of-fit test ranks first on this data (W p = 0.8176, the highest of six). Here is what it does. Quote any one of these alone and you will mislead someone:

#MeasurementWhat it says on its own
i 0.0% contamination movement. Inflate the largest loss 5× (or 10×) and the parameters do not move at all. “Perfect robustness.” True, and the trim genuinely never reads the corrupted point.
ii 62.94% resample instability. Delete the top observation and the 1-in-100 moves 62.94%; at k = 2, 47.21%; at k = 3, 61.32%. “The worst of the four contenders” — worse than the untrimmed MLE it was supposed to beat.
iii A clean 1-in-100 of 4.08e7 $bn (≈ $4.1e16) — about 5.6e5× the largest event ever recorded — because multiplies σ̂ in the exponent. “The level is meaningless.” And it passes every diagnostic an analyst would normally run.

The mechanism — why (i) and (ii) are not a contradiction. The two stresses act through different channels. Inflation changes a value the trimmed grid never reads, so nothing propagates: the influence function is bounded and the breakdown point does its job. Deletion changes which order statistics the fixed probability levels land on — the estimator is asked for the same probability levels of a genuinely different sample — so σ̂ moves a little. And then sits in the exponent and turns “a little” into a factor: a 0.04 move in σ̂ multiplies the return level by about 3.6.

Robust-to-outliers ≠ robust-to-resampling. A bounded influence function protects the parameters; only a bounded quantile transform protects the decision. That sentence is the study's single most transferable result.

And the metrology said it first. The decision track's paired log-return-level bootstrap SE is 3.461, of which 3.59 comes from the log-Cauchy arm alone — a standard error whose literal 2× on the return-level scale exceeds the metric's own range. At n = 30 this family's 1-in-100 is not estimable to useful precision, and the floor sweep in adaptive-1 knew that before the ranking in adaptive-3 confirmed it.

One consequence the code enforces rather than documents. log-Cauchy has no finite moments. In this study's implementation mean_loss() and cte() raise NotImplementedError by design. A numerical CTE would return a large float that is an artefact of where the quadrature was truncated and would look exactly like an estimate. An actuary who follows the goodness-of-fit test into a TVaR-loaded premium gets a confident number with no meaning; the refusal converts a silent pricing error into a stack trace.

The breakdown-point ladder

Read the solid line first: 58.06 → 41.33 → 27.58 as the breakdown point goes 0 → 0.05 → 0.10. Monotone, and the total move of 30.479351 pp is 30.5× the ordering delta. Now read the dashed line: on deletion alone it is 44.56 → 41.33 → 27.58. The MLE's binding stress is contamination; for gQLS, contamination is free and deletion is the whole cost.

The most striking rung is the leave-top-1 stress specifically, which collapses 19.02% → 5.66% between the two trims. Widening the trim by five percentage points at each end cut the single most likely real-world perturbation by more than two-thirds.

Why the cliff is missing, and why that is informative. The registered prediction was that k = 2 (2/30 = 0.067, past BP = 0.05) would breach the narrow trim. It cost only +3.70 pp relative to the wider trim. The reason is that guarantees an estimate cannot be driven arbitrarily by corruption in place — and deletion is a different stress, because dropping the top k re-indexes every order statistic. The cliff-shaped guarantee shows up on the stress BP actually governs: under 5× inflation of the maximum, both gQLS trims sit at exactly 0.0% while the MLE moves +58.06%.

And the trim was free here. Prediction 8 budgeted a goodness-of-fit cost for the wider trim. Instead W p rose from 0.5153 to 0.7310 (matching the published 0.73). The mechanism: W is scored only on the estimation grid, so a wider trim scores a wider interval — the very structural flaw the universal W_out grid exists to fix — and the extreme levels a narrow trim reaches are precisely where a 30-point sample is noisiest. Check this on your own data rather than assuming it: the classical efficiency argument says a wider trim must cost fit, and on this sample it did not.

Surprises

  1. The thesis uses two different quantile conventions in one chapter, and never says so. Section 4 tells it as a detective story. The generalizable form is C7 in section 6: a gQLS specification is five things — (family, a, b, k, quantile convention) — and the last two are usually left to a library default.
  2. The reproduction localized a typo in the published table — and proved it was a typo. Table 6.9's (0.10, 0.90) log-Gumbel μ̂ prints 22.34; our fit gives 22.358687; Table 6.10 prints 22.36 for the same fit, 0.0013 from ours. The reproduction does not disagree with the thesis — it disagrees with one printing and agrees with the other.
  3. The published p-values are too close to a χ² reference to have come from the prescribed bootstrap. 16 of 18 within 0.005, when a B=1000 bootstrap's own MC standard error near p = 0.5 is ≈0.016 and our honest bootstrap wanders from χ² by mean 0.0814. Agreement that tight is not something a bootstrap can produce. Stated as a reproducibility observation about printed numbers, and sent to section 7 as a question rather than an erratum.
  4. The best-fitting family is perfectly contamination-robust and catastrophically resample-unstable at the same time — 0.0% and 62.94% from the same fit. Detailed above; it is the study's headline.
  5. k moves the estimates more than the convention does, and both are ~20× the reporting resolution. Max parameter movement 0.1223 across k ∈ {8, 10, 15, 25} versus 0.1040 across six conventions. The mechanism: at n = 30, asking for k = 25 distinct quantile levels on (0.05, 0.95) requests more resolution than 30 order statistics contain — levels collapse onto repeated order statistics and the design matrix starts describing ties rather than the distribution. (The measurement reports the k ≤ 15 restriction separately and it is the same 0.1223, so this is not a k = 25 artefact.)
  6. Widening the trim made the in-sample fit better, not worse — and one “over-resolution” sealed cell turned out to be a rounding boundary (g2*/log-Gumbel: our unchanged σ̂ = 0.734805 printed as 0.73 in one column and 0.74 in the next). Two different reproduction “failures”, both resolved by looking at what was typeset. A pleasing confirmation in the same grid: Pareto I's contaminated σ̂ moves by exactly log(10)/30 = 0.076753, with μ̂ unchanged because its MLE is the boundary estimator — the method card predicted the number before the sealed run produced it.

What it is worth

The value proposition as one number a committee can act on: the difference between the two methods is a doubling of the modelled 100-year loss, caused by one bad row that no data-quality process caught. For a catastrophe layer, a capital charge, or a solvency return, a 100% error in the 1-in-100 is not a modelling nuance; it is the difference between two different companies. Quantile trimming is, in this specific and quantifiable sense, data-quality insurance: it costs a defined amount of statistical efficiency and it pays out exactly when a record is wrong.

And the honest version is more useful than the inflated one. The +99.4% and the 0.0% are properties of this fixed 30-event sample under this stress. σ̂ carries a ±25% bootstrap band at n = 30, and the 1.0 pp minimum_delta is an ordering device, not a confidence statement. What transfers is not “gQLS gives you a 0.0% number”; it is the structure — an estimator that never reads the corrupted observation cannot be moved by it, and an estimator that reads every observation can be moved arbitrarily. The first is a theorem; the second is arithmetic. The sizing on your own book is a measurement you must repeat.

The counter-lesson costs more to learn the hard way. The best-fitting family by the thesis's own test produces a 1-in-100 of $4.08e7bn — roughly 400× world GDP — while passing every diagnostic an analyst would normally run and being perfectly robust at the parameter level. An analyst who selects a severity family by GoF p-value and quotes its 1-in-100 has done everything the textbook asks and produced a number with no economic content. Three cheap guardrails would have caught it: refuse moments on moment-free families; print the return level next to the empirical maximum; and treat any extrapolation more than an order of magnitude beyond observed support as a modelling failure rather than a tail estimate. The GoF test answers “does this shape fit the middle of my data”; it does not answer “is the tail I am about to sell defensible”.

Adoption economics. A method published in an August 2024 dissertation was implemented from scratch (six families, two estimators, two goodness-of-fit tests, a parametric bootstrap), validated against 156 published parameters (36 adaptive + 120 sealed) to a mean deviation of about 0.002, stressed beyond anything the source publishes, carried into the decision unit the source explicitly names as future work, and closed with a full audit trail — inside one session and 11 experiments, with total measured compute of 7.7 seconds. The transferable claim is not that gQLS is cheap to run. It is that the evaluation of a frontier method is now cheap enough that “we don't know if it works on our data” has stopped being a reason to defer it. The binding constraint is whether anyone writes down, in advance, what would have to be true for the method to be rejected. That is what the eight registered predictions and the two sealed accesses bought — and three of the eight came back refuted, including the one the study existed to ask.

Limitations

Carried in the spirit of the data card's own wording rules, which were written before any modeling and are quoted where they are load-bearing.

  1. n = 30, and one observation dominates every tail fit. “max = 72.303 is 2.185× the second-largest (33.094) … Parametric bootstrap at n = 30 puts σ̂ = 0.8339 in a 95% interval of [0.617, 1.034] — a ±25% relative sampling band on the single parameter that drives the tail.” Consequently the 1.0 pp minimum_delta is “far smaller than the ±25% band on the estimand — treat it as an ordering device between estimators on a fixed sample, never as a claim about the population.” Every decision-track number above is such an ordering.
  2. The decision functional is out-of-sample by construction. “The largest of 30 sits at plotting position p = 0.9667 ≈ 1-in-30. Every 1-in-100 quantile is therefore pure parametric extrapolation … the fitted lognormal 1-in-100 (55.52 bn) is below the largest observation actually seen (72.303 bn) — the ‘100-year loss’ is an artifact of the assumed family, not a data-supported number.” The four GoF-passing 1-in-100s here span nearly six orders of magnitude — $53.04bn, $55.52bn, $56.66bn and $4.08e7bn — on the same data.
  3. The sealed evidence is independent TARGETS, not independent DATA. “the same 30 observations underlie both the adaptive and the sealed evaluation … it establishes implementation fidelity + within-sample robustness, never out-of-sample generalization. Sampling error is fully common-mode between adaptive and sealed evaluation and the sealed test cannot detect it.” A residual soft coupling also remains — a specification tuned until it hits Table 6.9 has thereby made much of Table 6.10 predictable — which the design bounds but does not eliminate.
  4. Stationarity is inherited, not established. The 30 events span 1900–1995 and are treated as exchangeable draws from one severity distribution solely because of the Pielke-Landsea normalization for inflation, wealth and coastal population. Anything that normalization fails to remove — building codes, warning lead times, insurance penetration, any trend in storm intensity — is absorbed silently into σ̂ and therefore into every return level here. The observed r(year, log damage) = −0.42 is equally consistent with sampling noise and with over-normalization, and cannot be distinguished at n = 30.
  5. The MLE baseline's GoF gate is heuristic. The w_pvalue ≥ 0.10 guardrail admitted E0006 on W p = 0.5374 with w_chi2_calibrated = 0: the calibration is established for gQLS residuals and the implementation correctly flags that it is not established for an MLE fit. The MLE arm passed a comparable-looking screen rather than a calibrated one. This does not affect C5's ordering (log-Cauchy loses on its own calibrated fit), but the MLE row of any comparison table should carry the flag.
  6. The k-sensitivity result is off-ledger. It is a program.md-recorded measurement with no run manifest and no experiment-level uncertainty. It was re-verified at synthesis and reproduces exactly (0.12227960638 across k; 0.10393799264 across conventions), but it is descriptive evidence and no verdict rests on it alone.
  7. The playbook was never filled — and that is a process finding, not a footnote. All four of playbook.md's tables remained at their scaffolded headers, so the pre-clustered discard map the synthesis protocol expects to mine did not exist; the synthesis reconstructed the clusters from program.md's decision log and the ledgers instead. All three phase acknowledgements recorded the same (scaffold) hash, so the gap was visible in the audit trail rather than hidden by it. The record is silent on why, and nothing is inferred. Two things are worth saying about it. First, the reconstruction worked because the journal and the ledgers are append-only and complete — the redundancy in the protocol is what made a missing artifact recoverable rather than fatal. Second, a future study on this ladder should treat the playbook as load-bearing: the reconstruction cost real synthesis effort and, on a longer study with more discards, would not have been possible at the same fidelity. Reporting the gap is cheaper than the gap.
  8. Two GoF-passing families were never carried into the decision track. log-Logistic and log-Laplace pass comfortably (W p 0.5349 and 0.5965 at (0.05, 0.95)) and their standard quantiles at p = 0.99 are bounded (4.60 and 3.91), so they are plausible middle rungs between lognormal (2.326) and log-Cauchy (31.82). The adaptive-3 budget went to the trim ladder instead. C6's “iff bounded” is therefore supported by a two-point contrast, not by a curve.
