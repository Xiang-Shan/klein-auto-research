The Method

Four things had to be taught before a single number could be believed: what the metric is, what "different" means on it, how to interrogate a black box for structure, and what a shape constraint actually constrains.

1. The metric — and why exposure is a weight, not an offset

The response is a rate: yi = ni / ei, claims divided by exposure-years, with the exposure ei serving as the fitting and scoring weight. Every model on both tracks is driven that way — no per-library offset plumbing anywhere.

unit Poisson deviance   d(y, mu) = 2 * ( y*log(y/mu) - y + mu ),      0*log 0 := 0

fold deviance           D(m) = SUM_i w_i * d(y_i, mu_m(x_i))  /  SUM_i w_i      (w_i = e_i)

the gap                 Delta = D(GLM) - D(GBDT)      -- positive means GBDT is better

That the exposure-weighted rate form is identical to the textbook offset form is the load-bearing step, so the method card derives it rather than citing it:

log Pois(n | e*mu) = n*log(e*mu) - e*mu - log n!
substitute n = e*y, drop theta-free terms   ->   e * ( y*log mu - mu )

and              e * d(y,mu) / 2  =  e * ( y*log y - y*log mu - y + mu )
whose theta-dependent part is        - e * ( y*log mu - mu )

=>   argmax SUM log Pois(n_i | e_i * mu_theta)  ==  argmin SUM e_i * d(y_i, mu_theta)
        (count likelihood, offset log e)            (exposure-weighted rate deviance)

both give the same score equation:
        SUM_i ( n_i - e_i * mu_theta(x_i) ) * grad_theta eta_theta(x_i) = 0

For a GBDT the equality holds at the level of the loss and its gradients, so it propagates into every split gain and every leaf value. This is why PoissonRegressor, HistGradientBoostingRegressor, LGBMRegressor(objective="poisson") and CatBoostRegressor(loss_function="Poisson") can all be handed the identical (y_rate, sample_weight=Exposure) pair and still be comparing like with like. One caveat worth carrying into a filing memo: the equivalence is for the fitted function; dispersion-based standard errors differ unless φ is handled consistently. Irrelevant here — the metric is a deviance, not a Wald test.

2. Three floors, not one — the metrology that governs every verdict

A delta is only evidence if it exceeds the noise of the comparison that produced it. This study measured four distinct floors and they differ by more than a factor of four. Getting this wrong is the single most common way a gap becomes a fiction.

FloorValueWhat it governs
glm fit-seed std (k = 5)exactly 0nothing — PoissonRegressor is deterministic. The degenerate value IS a finding: quoting a seed floor for a GLM is meaningless.
gbdt fit-seed std (k = 5)0.000210HGBT re-fit reproducibility only
glm paired-bootstrap SE (B = 1000, CRN)0.000270 → minimum_delta 0.000539within-glm-track comparisons
gbdt paired-bootstrap SE (B = 1000, CRN)0.000287 → minimum_delta 0.000573within-gbdt-track comparisons
cross-track paired SE (development)0.000963the headline GLM↔GBDT gap on development
cross-track paired SE (sealed)0.001028the headline gap on the sealed test

Why the paired floor is the one that governs. A seed floor asks "if I re-fit this model, how much does its score move?" — which says nothing about whether two different models differ. The paired bootstrap asks the right question: resample the evaluation rows once with common random numbers, score both models on the same resample, and take the standard deviation of the difference across B = 1000 draws. Shared row-level noise cancels; only the disagreement between the models survives. Bootstrapping the two levels separately and differencing their SEs would overstate the comparison noise several-fold.

Note the asymmetry that follows: the cross-track SE (0.000963) is 4.6× the GBDT seed floor and is the only one of the four that legitimately bands the headline gap. It is an uncertainty band, not a minimum_delta — it never gates a keep/discard decision. Track frontiers are gated by the within-track deltas; the gap is reported against the cross-track SE.

3. The forensics instruments — how to ask a black box where

Three tools, all off-ledger (descriptive analysis, never evidence), all reading artifacts the sanctioned loop already produced.

M4 — train-fold surrogate distillation

Knowledge distillation trains a small student on a big teacher's outputs. Here the machinery is repurposed as a measurement instrument, and that changes how you read it:

The GBDT's log-prediction surface is a smooth function of the rating factors. Fit a GLM-shaped surrogate — main effects only, in a basis the real GLM could actually use — to that surface. Whatever the surrogate cannot reproduce is, by construction, the part of the teacher's structure that is not representable as GLM main effects. Correlate that residual against candidate two-way products and the largest correlations name which interactions the GBDT found.
teacher's log-score      F(x)     = log mu_GBDT(x)      -- TRAIN fold only
main-effects surrogate   g(x; th) = per-column cubic basis (x, x^2, x^3), weighted LS

th_hat = argmin_th  SUM_{i in TRAIN}  w_i * ( F(x_i) - g(x_i; th) )^2

r_i    = F(x_i) - g(x_i; th_hat)                   <-- the non-additive remainder

R2_main   = 1 - SUM w*r^2 / SUM w*(F - Fbar_w)^2

score(a,b) = | SUM_i w_i * z_i^ab * r_i / SUM_i w_i |
             ---------------------------------------------   in [0, 1]
                sqrt( SUM_i w_i * r_i^2 / SUM_i w_i )

score(a,b) is the weighted correlation between the standardised product column zab and the residual. Five numeric factors give C(5,2) = 10 candidate pairs; rank them, promote the top-K into the real GLM as design-matrix columns, and measure development deviance. The instrument and the intervention are the same object — the column that scored highest IS the column you add, so a screening hit is immediately falsifiable by one refit.

Three properties make this a screen rather than a fishing expedition:

  1. It never touches evaluation labels. The surrogate's target is the teacher's train-fold predictions, not y. Derivation lives entirely inside train; development is spent only on the adopt/reject decision; the sealed fold confirms.
  2. Multiplicity is fixed in advance and published. 10 screened, at most 2 adopted, both counts reported either way. A screen you report is a screen; a screen you hide is p-hacking.
  3. R²_main is itself a headline number. It answers RQ5's real question — is the gap an interaction gap at all? — before a single interaction is adopted.

Read it correctly. R²_main lives on the log-score surface, not on deviance. It is a diagnostic, not a bound on recoverable deviance. A high value with a stubborn gap would have meant the gap lives in basis/knot curvature the scoped splines do not span — a different, equally reportable answer.

M4 cross-check — the manual two-way partial dependence

A single instrument that names an interaction is a hypothesis; two instruments agreeing is evidence. The cross-check is model-agnostic and computed by hand on the log scale (GLM additivity is log-scale additivity):

strength(a,b) = std[  PD2(a,b)  -  ( PD1(a) + PD1(b) )  ]

              all surfaces mean-centred, 8x8 quantile grid, evaluated on log(mu)

If a pair is genuinely additive, the 2-D partial-dependence surface equals the sum of the two 1-D curves and the residual surface is flat. Its standard deviation is the pair's non-additivity in log-rate units. A pair is adopted only if the surrogate ranking and the PD signature agree on it.

M5 — segment deviance decomposition

The weighted deviance is a sum of per-row contributions, so any disjoint partition of the evaluation rows partitions the total exactly — no cross terms, no residual, nothing approximated:

D(m)  = SUM_k D_k(m)          for ANY disjoint partition {S_1 ... S_K} of the eval rows

Delta = SUM_k [ D_k(GLM) - D_k(GBDT) ] = SUM_k Delta_k

gap share_k = Delta_k / Delta                         SUM_k gap share_k = 1

That identity is what converts a leaderboard row into a WHERE answer. "72% of the gap sits in three segments covering 11% of exposure" is a completely different business story from "the gap is spread evenly across the book" — and both produce the same headline number. Discipline: individual Δk may be negative (segments where the GLM wins), so shares can exceed 1; always print each segment's exposure share next to its gap share, or a 1%-exposure segment will look decisive when it is noise.

4. Monotone constraints — and the distinction that explains their price

A monotone constraint is not a post-hoc smoothing. It is enforced inside split finding: every node carries a value band [ℓ, u] inherited from its ancestors, and a split on the constrained feature is admissible only if

v_left <= v_right ,    with  v_left, v_right  in  [l, u]

              (the children's bands are tightened so the ordering
               can never be undone deeper in the tree)

Additive stagewise composition preserves the property, so the whole ensemble inherits the guarantee scikit-learn states as x1 ≤ x1' ⇒ F(x1, x2) ≤ F(x1', x2).

Read that guarantee slowly, because it is the entire finding of RQ7. It is quantified over every value of x2 — i.e. monotonicity is enforced pointwise in every conditioning context, not on the marginal curve. So:

Marginal monotonicity does not imply conditional monotonicity, and it is the conditional version the library enforces.

A factor can be perfectly non-decreasing when you plot mean frequency against it, and the constraint can still be expensive — because it forbids the model from letting that factor's slope vary with another factor. Section 5 shows exactly that happening, for exactly the reason the surrogate screen predicted.

Encoding note: +1 increase, 0 unconstrained, -1 decrease, one entry per input feature. Categorical features cannot be constrained (categories are unordered), so hgbt_monotone puts 0 on every categorical position and +1 on BonusMalus only — which is why that config also switches to the native-categorical encoder, and why RQ7 needed a same-encoding control.
