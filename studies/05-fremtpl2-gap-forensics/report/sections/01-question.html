The Question

A pricing actuary on a French motor third-party liability book has a boosted model that scores better than the filed GLM. Everyone knows this. The question that actually reaches a committee is narrower and harder:

How much accuracy are we giving up by filing the interpretable model — and is that number real, or is it an artefact of the fold we happened to measure it on?

This study answers it three ways at once. Its goal statement:

On freMTPL2 claim frequency, WHERE does a gradient-boosted model's Poisson-deviance advantage over a spline-shaped GLM live, how much of it transfers back into GLM-representable structure, and does the gap itself survive two sealed final-test evaluations?

Why "two sealed evaluations" is the whole design

Klein studies seal a final-test partition and allow each research track exactly one access to it. Everything else — every candidate, every discard, every tuning decision — happens on train and development only. The sealed number is therefore an honest out-of-sample confirmation rather than the last of many peeks.

Its predecessor, study 04, ran a single track. It could seal the level of one model, but the GLM→GBDT gap was still a development quantity, and it filed a protocol caveat saying so. This study splits the work into two tracks with independent frontiers:

  • glm — the interpretable family: OHE anchor, column-scoped splines, surrogate-derived interaction products.
  • gbdt — the black box: sklearn HGBT, LightGBM, CatBoost, and a monotone-constrained filability probe.

Each track selects its own incumbent adaptively on development, then spends its one sealed access on that incumbent. The headline gap becomes a difference of two sealed numbers — neither of which was chosen after looking at the sealed fold. That is the protocol contribution, and it is what closes study 04's caveat.

The two tracks are never merged into one leaderboard. Each owns its metric contract, its measured minimum_delta, and its guardrails:

The seven research questions

Each was registered with a prior before the loop ran, so each could come out wrong. Several did.

RQQuestionRegistered prior
RQ1Does the gap survive as a difference of two SEALED test numbers, within 2 paired-bootstrap SEs of the development gap (0.010172)?Yes — study 04's level replicated at 0.65× fold SE; the gap should too
RQ2Is LightGBM-Poisson within 2× the gbdt floor of sklearn HGBT at matched capacity?Yes, a tie (knowledge/gbdt-tabular.md)
RQ3Does CatBoost's ordered-target-statistics package beat OHE-HGBT at cardinality ≤ 22?No — study 04 found native-vs-OHE a 0.37×-floor tie here
RQ4With column-SCOPED splines, what fraction of the gap does additive shaping close?30–45%, up from study 04's leaky 18%
RQ5How much of the residual gap do the top surrogate-derived interactions recover?A further 20–30% (uninformed)
RQ6At what training-set size does the gap fall below 2× the paired floor?~10–15% of train, 40–60k rows (uninformed)
RQ7Does a monotone BonusMalus constraint cost more than 1× the gbdt floor?No — "filability is nearly free" (knowledge/glm-pricing.md)

RQ1 is the estimand. RQ4–RQ5 are the translate-back programme: can the black box's advantage be handed back to a filable model? RQ6 asks whether the whole question is even live at your portfolio's size. RQ7 prices the regulator's shape guarantee. RQ2–RQ3 clear the ground so no one can attribute the gap to a library choice.
