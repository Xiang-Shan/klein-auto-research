The Question

A pricing actuary on a French motor third-party liability book has a boosted model that scores better than the filed GLM. Everyone knows this. The question that actually reaches a committee is narrower and harder:

How much accuracy are we giving up by filing the interpretable model — and is that number real, or is it an artefact of the fold we happened to measure it on?

This study answers it three ways at once. Its goal statement:

On freMTPL2 claim frequency, WHERE does a gradient-boosted model's Poisson-deviance advantage over a spline-shaped GLM live, how much of it transfers back into GLM-representable structure, and does the gap itself survive two sealed final-test evaluations?

Why "two sealed evaluations" is the whole design

Klein studies seal a final-test partition and allow each research track exactly one access to it. Everything else — every candidate, every discard, every tuning decision — happens on train and development only. The sealed number is therefore an honest out-of-sample confirmation rather than the last of many peeks.

Its predecessor, study 04, ran a single track. It could seal the level of one model, but the GLM→GBDT gap was still a development quantity, and it filed a protocol caveat saying so. This study splits the work into two tracks with independent frontiers:

  • glm — the interpretable family: OHE anchor, column-scoped splines, surrogate-derived interaction products.
  • gbdt — the black box: sklearn HGBT, LightGBM, CatBoost, and a monotone-constrained filability probe.

Each track selects its own incumbent adaptively on development, then spends its one sealed access on that incumbent. The headline gap becomes a difference of two sealed numbers — neither of which was chosen after looking at the sealed fold. That is the protocol contribution, and it is what closes study 04's caveat.

The two tracks are never merged into one leaderboard. Each owns its metric contract, its measured minimum_delta, and its guardrails:

The Method

Four things had to be taught before a single number could be believed: what the metric is, what "different" means on it, how to interrogate a black box for structure, and what a shape constraint actually constrains.

1. The metric — and why exposure is a weight, not an offset

The response is a rate: yi = ni / ei, claims divided by exposure-years, with the exposure ei serving as the fitting and scoring weight. Every model on both tracks is driven that way — no per-library offset plumbing anywhere.

unit Poisson deviance   d(y, mu) = 2 * ( y*log(y/mu) - y + mu ),      0*log 0 := 0

fold deviance           D(m) = SUM_i w_i * d(y_i, mu_m(x_i))  /  SUM_i w_i      (w_i = e_i)

the gap                 Delta = D(GLM) - D(GBDT)      -- positive means GBDT is better

That the exposure-weighted rate form is identical to the textbook offset form is the load-bearing step, so the method card derives it rather than citing it:

log Pois(n | e*mu) = n*log(e*mu) - e*mu - log n!
substitute n = e*y, drop theta-free terms   ->   e * ( y*log mu - mu )

and              e * d(y,mu) / 2  =  e * ( y*log y - y*log mu - y + mu )
whose theta-dependent part is        - e * ( y*log mu - mu )

=>   argmax SUM log Pois(n_i | e_i * mu_theta)  ==  argmin SUM e_i * d(y_i, mu_theta)
        (count likelihood, offset log e)            (exposure-weighted rate deviance)

both give the same score equation:
        SUM_i ( n_i - e_i * mu_theta(x_i) ) * grad_theta eta_theta(x_i) = 0

For a GBDT the equality holds at the level of the loss and its gradients, so it propagates into every split gain and every leaf value. This is why PoissonRegressor, HistGradientBoostingRegressor, LGBMRegressor(objective="poisson") and CatBoostRegressor(loss_function="Poisson") can all be handed the identical (y_rate, sample_weight=Exposure) pair and still be comparing like with like. One caveat worth carrying into a filing memo: the equivalence is for the fitted function; dispersion-based standard errors differ unless φ is handled consistently. Irrelevant here — the metric is a deviance, not a Wald test.

2. Three floors, not one — the metrology that governs every verdict

A delta is only evidence if it exceeds the noise of the comparison that produced it. This study measured four distinct floors and they differ by more than a factor of four. Getting this wrong is the single most common way a gap becomes a fiction.

FloorValueWhat it governs
glm fit-seed std (k = 5)exactly 0nothing — PoissonRegressor is deterministic. The degenerate value IS a finding: quoting a seed floor for a GLM is meaningless.
gbdt fit-seed std (k = 5)0.000210HGBT re-fit reproducibility only
glm paired-bootstrap SE (B = 1000, CRN)0.000270 → minimum_delta 0.000539within-glm-track comparisons
gbdt paired-bootstrap SE (B = 1000, CRN)0.000287 → minimum_delta 0.000573within-gbdt-track comparisons
cross-track paired SE (development)0.000963the headline GLM↔GBDT gap on development
cross-track paired SE (sealed)0.001028the headline gap on the sealed test

Why the paired floor is the one that governs. A seed floor asks "if I re-fit this model, how much does its score move?" — which says nothing about whether two different models differ. The paired bootstrap asks the right question: resample the evaluation rows once with common random numbers, score both models on the same resample, and take the standard deviation of the difference across B = 1000 draws. Shared row-level noise cancels; only the disagreement between the models survives. Bootstrapping the two levels separately and differencing their SEs would overstate the comparison noise several-fold.

Note the asymmetry that follows: the cross-track SE (0.000963) is 4.6× the GBDT seed floor and is the only one of the four that legitimately bands the headline gap. It is an uncertainty band, not a minimum_delta — it never gates a keep/discard decision. Track frontiers are gated by the within-track deltas; the gap is reported against the cross-track SE.

3. The forensics instruments — how to ask a black box where

Three tools, all off-ledger (descriptive analysis, never evidence), all reading artifacts the sanctioned loop already produced.

M4 — train-fold surrogate distillation

Knowledge distillation trains a small student on a big teacher's outputs. Here the machinery is repurposed as a measurement instrument, and that changes how you read it:

The GBDT's log-prediction surface is a smooth function of the rating factors. Fit a GLM-shaped surrogate — main effects only, in a basis the real GLM could actually use — to that surface. Whatever the surrogate cannot reproduce is, by construction, the part of the teacher's structure that is not representable as GLM main effects. Correlate that residual against candidate two-way products and the largest correlations name which interactions the GBDT found.
teacher's log-score      F(x)     = log mu_GBDT(x)      -- TRAIN fold only
main-effects surrogate   g(x; th) = per-column cubic basis (x, x^2, x^3), weighted LS

th_hat = argmin_th  SUM_{i in TRAIN}  w_i * ( F(x_i) - g(x_i; th) )^2

r_i    = F(x_i) - g(x_i; th_hat)                   <-- the non-additive remainder

R2_main   = 1 - SUM w*r^2 / SUM w*(F - Fbar_w)^2

score(a,b) = | SUM_i w_i * z_i^ab * r_i / SUM_i w_i |
             ---------------------------------------------   in [0, 1]
                sqrt( SUM_i w_i * r_i^2 / SUM_i w_i )

score(a,b) is the weighted correlation between the standardised product column zab and the residual. Five numeric factors give C(5,2) = 10 candidate pairs; rank them, promote the top-K into the real GLM as design-matrix columns, and measure development deviance. The instrument and the intervention are the same object — the column that scored highest IS the column you add, so a screening hit is immediately falsifiable by one refit.

Three properties make this a screen rather than a fishing expedition:

  1. It never touches evaluation labels. The surrogate's target is the teacher's train-fold predictions, not y. Derivation lives entirely inside train; development is spent only on the adopt/reject decision; the sealed fold confirms.
  2. Multiplicity is fixed in advance and published. 10 screened, at most 2 adopted, both counts reported either way. A screen you report is a screen; a screen you hide is p-hacking.
  3. R²_main is itself a headline number. It answers RQ5's real question — is the gap an interaction gap at all? — before a single interaction is adopted.

Read it correctly. R²_main lives on the log-score surface, not on deviance. It is a diagnostic, not a bound on recoverable deviance. A high value with a stubborn gap would have meant the gap lives in basis/knot curvature the scoped splines do not span — a different, equally reportable answer.

M4 cross-check — the manual two-way partial dependence

A single instrument that names an interaction is a hypothesis; two instruments agreeing is evidence. The cross-check is model-agnostic and computed by hand on the log scale (GLM additivity is log-scale additivity):

strength(a,b) = std[  PD2(a,b)  -  ( PD1(a) + PD1(b) )  ]

              all surfaces mean-centred, 8x8 quantile grid, evaluated on log(mu)

If a pair is genuinely additive, the 2-D partial-dependence surface equals the sum of the two 1-D curves and the residual surface is flat. Its standard deviation is the pair's non-additivity in log-rate units. A pair is adopted only if the surrogate ranking and the PD signature agree on it.

M5 — segment deviance decomposition

The weighted deviance is a sum of per-row contributions, so any disjoint partition of the evaluation rows partitions the total exactly — no cross terms, no residual, nothing approximated:

D(m)  = SUM_k D_k(m)          for ANY disjoint partition {S_1 ... S_K} of the eval rows

Delta = SUM_k [ D_k(GLM) - D_k(GBDT) ] = SUM_k Delta_k

gap share_k = Delta_k / Delta                         SUM_k gap share_k = 1

That identity is what converts a leaderboard row into a WHERE answer. "72% of the gap sits in three segments covering 11% of exposure" is a completely different business story from "the gap is spread evenly across the book" — and both produce the same headline number. Discipline: individual Δk may be negative (segments where the GLM wins), so shares can exceed 1; always print each segment's exposure share next to its gap share, or a 1%-exposure segment will look decisive when it is noise.

4. Monotone constraints — and the distinction that explains their price

A monotone constraint is not a post-hoc smoothing. It is enforced inside split finding: every node carries a value band [ℓ, u] inherited from its ancestors, and a split on the constrained feature is admissible only if

v_left <= v_right ,    with  v_left, v_right  in  [l, u]

              (the children's bands are tightened so the ordering
               can never be undone deeper in the tree)

Additive stagewise composition preserves the property, so the whole ensemble inherits the guarantee scikit-learn states as x1 ≤ x1' ⇒ F(x1, x2) ≤ F(x1', x2).

Read that guarantee slowly, because it is the entire finding of RQ7. It is quantified over every value of x2 — i.e. monotonicity is enforced pointwise in every conditioning context, not on the marginal curve. So:

Marginal monotonicity does not imply conditional monotonicity, and it is the conditional version the library enforces.

A factor can be perfectly non-decreasing when you plot mean frequency against it, and the constraint can still be expensive — because it forbids the model from letting that factor's slope vary with another factor. Section 5 shows exactly that happening, for exactly the reason the surrogate screen predicted.

Encoding note: +1 increase, 0 unconstrained, -1 decrease, one entry per input feature. Categorical features cannot be constrained (categories are unordered), so hgbt_monotone puts 0 on every categorical position and +1 on BonusMalus only — which is why that config also switches to the native-categorical encoder, and why RQ7 needed a same-encoding control.

The value-pattern gotchas (checked against raw tokens, never dtypes)

Klein's standing war story is that dtype == "object" checks lie under pandas string dtypes, so the audit reads raw CSV tokens with dtype=str, na_filter=False. All 12 columns, by value:

  • VehGas is a two-level string binary — exactly {Regular: 345,877, Diesel: 332,136}. Not Yes/No, not 0/1. This is the exact shape that silently skipped categorical handling in the ancestor campaign. It is listed in pipeline.CATEGORICAL, so OHE picks it up — no dtype-based skip.
  • VehBrand and Region are digits-inside-strings (B12, R24). They must never be coerced to numbers.
  • Density has a real censoring ceiling — 10,515 rows (1.55%) pinned at exactly 27,000, so p99 == max. A genuine ceiling in the source, not a sentinel; never treat it as missing. Skew 4.65 → 0.05 after log1p, which is why the shaped GLM configs log it first.
  • The SENTINEL regex fired twice and both were false positives — the substring 999 inside legitimate IDpol/Density integers, and the letter F as an Area level. Dismissed by inspecting the full value sets.
  • IDpol is a perfect row identifier (678,013 unique, monotone increasing, r = −0.0396 with the target). Structurally excluded — X is built from the 9 modelled columns only.
  • The exposure lower clip silently binds. 1,060 rows sit at exactly repr(1/365.25) — the 19-decimal fingerprint of prepare.py's clip — while the hygiene counter reports 0, because it only counts rows outside (0, 1]. Recorded, not fixed: prep is frozen.
  • The ClaimNb cap at 4 is a no-op here. 0 rows exceed 4 pre-clip; 13 rows sit at the cap. The hub table arrives already capped — the guard is inherited, not applied.
  • Zero missing values, zero whitespace padding, zero mixed-type columns, zero true sentinels.

The dominant caveat: feature-profile twins straddle the split

The 9 modelled features repeat heavily — 528,765 distinct profiles over 678,013 rows. Under the seed-42 split, 31,798 profile groups span train and development, so:

26.54% of development rows (35,985 / 135,603) and 26.37% of sealed-test rows have a feature-identical twin in the train fold.

These are not full-row duplicates — IDpol is unique and Exposure varies, so the mechanized duplicate-row check correctly passes. They are policies with identical rating characteristics. The decision was to record, not fix: deduplicating or switching to a group split would break the 0.454861 / 0.444689 anchors and destroy cross-study comparability.

This is exactly why the study's standing directive is report the GAP, never the levels. Both tracks consume identical contamination, so the GLM↔GBDT difference — the actual estimand — is unbiased by it; what is optimistic is every absolute deviance versus a fresh portfolio. The upside is also bounded: twin rows are claim-poor (1.97% claim-bearing among the dev twins vs 3.68% overall).

The other reason levels lie: the folds are not identically frequent

Exposure-weighted frequency by partition:

train  0.073900          development  0.072375          sealed test  0.074350

A model calibrated on train therefore must over-predict development by 0.073900 / 0.072375 = 1.021 and under-predict the sealed test by 0.073900 / 0.074350 = 0.994 — before any question of model quality. Hold that thought; section 5 shows it explaining an apparent calibration finding almost exactly.

Go / no-go

Decision: GO-WITH-CAUTIONS. No BLOCKER open. Three WARNs carried forward as standing directives: (1) report the gap, never the levels; (2) derive interaction pairs from a train-fold surrogate only — the pairs are a caller-supplied argument, which is the one live lookahead surface no static check can catch; (3) never filter the 365× rate tail — exposure weighting already down-weights a one-day policy to 1/365 of a full-year policy.

One structural limitation the card flags for the findings: the prepared artifact has no time column, so a temporal split is impossible and the frozen random split is the only realizable design. Every conclusion here is an in-period statement about this portfolio, not a forward-in-time generalization.

Findings

Seven claims carry the study. One is confirmed against sealed evidence; the rest are development-fold findings and say so. Claim IDs are stable (05-fremtpl2-gap-forensics#C<n>) and are never renumbered.

C1 — the gap survives as a difference of two sealed numbers [confirmed]

sealed gap   0.459231 - 0.449667 = +0.009564  =  9.3x the sealed cross-track SE (0.001028)

dev gap      0.453156 - 0.444689 = +0.008467       (incumbent vs incumbent)
| sealed - dev |                 =  0.001097  =  1.07x SE   -> inside the 2-SE band

anchor gap   0.454861 - 0.444689 = +0.010172  =  10.6x the dev cross-track SE (0.000963)

This is the study's estimand and both tracks' single sealed accesses were spent on it. The pre-registered band was "within 2 paired SEs of the development gap"; the observed displacement is 1.07 SE. The anchor-config gap — the reference the whole study was designed around — replicates too. Study 04's protocol caveat, that a gap cannot be sealed-confirmed under one-access-per-track, is closed.

C4 — additive shaping tops out near 17%, and the leak was never the constraint

Column-scoped splines on DrivAge, BonusMalus and log-Density moved the GLM 0.454861 → 0.453156: −0.001705, a real kept improvement at 3.2× the glm floor — and only 16.8% of the 0.010172 anchor gap, less than half the 30–45% prior.

The decisive sub-finding is a comparison, not a level. Study 04's shaped GLM had a leaky basis (the spline transform spilled onto the OHE dummies) and closed 17.6%. This study's clean, column-scoped basis — the whole reason RQ4 existed — closed 16.8%.

Fixing the leak changed the closure by −0.8 percentage points. The leak was never the binding constraint. Mechanism: splining a dummy is a no-op in expressiveness — a 0/1 column's cubic basis spans the same two-point space — so the "leak" added parameters and a hair of overfit, never signal.

C5 — 83% of the gap is non-additive residue: 10 screened, 0 adopted

The surrogate said the structure exists (R²_main = 0.6647) and named where it is. The GLM could not use it:

The honest zero is the finding. A floor-free search over ten pairs would have reported the best of them as an improvement; the multiplicity budget was fixed in advance (10 screened, ≤ 2 adopted, nothing adopted below 1× floor, every rejection reported) and it came in at 10 / 0 with room to spare.

Surprise inside the surprise: the interaction the literature would bet on — BonusMalus × DrivAge, the canonical French-MTPL confound where young drivers enter at the base level 100 — ranked 8 of 10. What the tree actually adds is vehicle-age conditioning of the experience-rating signal: an old car with a bad BM record is not the linear sum of "old car" and "bad record". The DrivAge–BonusMalus relationship is largely marginal confounding, which additive main effects already absorb.

Two honesty caveats, both load-bearing. Second: the 15% cell sits 0.000251 = 0.26× SE below the 10% cell, which is noise — describe the curve as "increasing apart from one sub-floor inversion", never as "monotonically increasing".

C7 — a filable monotone constraint costs 3.7× the floor even though it is true

This is the study's sharpest mechanism finding, and it only exists because two instruments were run in the right order.

M3-a first:  BonusMalus is non-decreasing in frequency over 100% of TRAIN EXPOSURE
             (marginally).  So +1 is not merely defensible - it is the right sign.

E0009 then:  constraint cost vs a SAME-ENCODING control (study 04 native HGBT 0.445343)
             0.447455 - 0.445343 = +0.002112  =  3.7x the gbdt floor

             vs the OHE incumbent (constraint + encoding switch)
             0.447455 - 0.444689 = +0.002766  =  4.8x floor

             effective_trees fell 67 -> 53: the constraint also cut capacity.

The prior said filability is nearly free. It is not. And because the marginal shape was checked first, the cost is interpretable rather than mysterious:

The cost is not a wrong-sign penalty. It is the price of forbidding conditional non-monotonicity. sklearn enforces the constraint pointwise in every conditioning context; the model wants the BonusMalus slope to bend differently for old vehicles than for new ones — precisely the VehAge×BonusMalus interaction that ranked first in the surrogate screen.

Two forensics instruments, run for unrelated reasons, landed on the same structure by different routes. That convergence is worth more than either number alone. And it is a completely different conversation with a regulator: not "your model wanted the wrong shape" but "your shape guarantee costs this much, and here is exactly which interaction it forbids".

C2 — the library tie, and the capacity trap underneath it

LightGBM-Poisson vs sklearn HGBT, at both capacities:

ComparisonDevianceΔ vs HGBT 0.444689× gbdt floor
nominal-matched (200 rounds, E0005)0.444413−0.0002760.48×
effective-matched (67 trees, E0010)0.444431−0.0002580.45×

A tie at both capacities, so the tie is algorithmic rather than an early-stopping artefact. Both are discards by the contract — and the discard IS the finding: library choice on this data is an operations decision (deployment target, native-categorical support, team familiarity), not an accuracy decision.

Underneath sits a trap worth more than the tie. HistGradientBoostingRegressor(max_iter=200) fitted 67 trees; LightGBM at n_estimators=200 built all 200; CatBoost built all 200; the monotone variant managed 53. Every "matched-capacity library comparison" published with those two default configs is comparing 67 trees to 200. Here the confound was real but inert — matching capacity made the delta shrink, because both libraries saturate this signal well before 67 trees. That is luck, not design: it was only visible because the method card forced effective_trees into the aux ledger on every GBDT row.

For completeness: CatBoost's CTR + symmetric-tree package (E0006, 0.446332) landed +2.9× floor worse than plain OHE-HGBT — a real deficit, not a tie. At ≤ 22 levels with ~9,000 train rows per Region level, ordered target statistics have nothing to de-bias. Open caveat: the isolation lever (one_hot_max_size=32) was never run, so CTR-vs-symmetric-tree attribution remains unresolved; the claim is package-level only.

The bonus finding: the "+2% calibration bias" was a fold, not a model

Every development row reports calibration_ratio ≈ 1.014–1.022 — a consistent 2% over-prediction that the method card froze as a known bias to be reported and never recalibrated. On the sealed test both models flip to under-prediction: 0.9962 (GLM, E0011) and 0.9905 (GBDT, E0012).

Nothing about either model changed. The data card's partition frequencies explain it almost exactly:

predicted over-prediction on dev   0.073900 / 0.072375 = 1.021    observed 1.0216
predicted under-prediction on test 0.073900 / 0.074350 = 0.994    observed 0.9962
The A/E number was measuring split sampling variation, not model calibration.

Both tracks move together, so the gap is untouched — which makes this the sharpest available demonstration of why "report the gap, never the levels" is the operative discipline. Practical consequence: the ±2% swing between folds is portfolio-sampling noise and should not be booked as a model loading. Both sealed models land within 1% of unity on A/E with no recalibration applied, so the deviance comparison is between two adequately-calibrated models rather than a rank-versus-calibration trade.

The scorecard — priors were right about decisions, wrong about magnitudes

Fourteen registered levers: 6 fully held (RQ1 replication, the RQ2 tie, M1-a's early stopping, M1-b's matched capacity, M3-a's monotone sign, M4-c's multiplicity budget); 2 held on disposition but falsified on magnitude (CatBoost's deficit is real, not a tie; splines keep but at half the predicted closure); 5 outright falsified (RQ5's further recovery, RQ7's free constraint, M4-a's additivity, M4-b's favourite pair, M5-a's concentration); 1 untested (the CatBoost isolation lever).

The study's priors were right about what the loop would decide far more often than about how much. Every magnitude prior that was stakeable came out wrong.

Worth noting for anyone maintaining a knowledge base: knowledge/-sourced priors went 1 fully held / 1 partially / 2 refuted, while uninformed priors went 1 held / 1 refuted. Informed priors did not outperform uninformed ones here, and both card refutations are cases where a large-sample, main-effects intuition was generalised into a regime where the binding structure was interactions.

What this means for a pricing committee

  • The price of interpretability is now a number with an error bar. A filed additive GLM — even a properly spline-shaped one — leaves 83.2% of the modelling gap on the table at this portfolio size. "Rebuild the GLM better" is not a route to closing it: the remaining advantage is non-additive structure a linear score cannot represent.
  • But the price is a function of portfolio size, and below ~40k rows it is zero or negative. For a small book, a new line, a thin segment carved out for separate rating, or a territory with sparse history, the boosted model buys nothing measurable and costs the whole governance, documentation and monitoring apparatus. Do not buy a GBDT programme for a portfolio that cannot feed it.
  • The shape guarantee has a price tag: 27% of the boosted edge. The unconstrained incumbent captures the full 0.010172 gap; the monotone variant captures 0.454861 − 0.447455 = 0.007406 = 72.8%. That is a concrete trade a committee can actually decide.
  • Where the gap does NOT live is as useful as where it does. The decomposition is diffuse — top-3 BonusMalus bins carry 87% of the gap on 89% of exposure. There is no thin high-frequency pocket where a specialist model earns its keep. Any capture of this gap is a book-wide re-rate, not a targeted intervention — which raises implementation cost and filing burden relative to the "just fix the bad segment" story the concentration prior would have supported.
  • Operational cost is not the deciding variable. Per fit: GLM 1.1–1.5 s, HGBT 2.9 s, LightGBM 2.2–3.2 s, CatBoost 7.1 s — all three orders of magnitude inside the 400 s guardrail.

Limitations, stated plainly

  1. Feature-profile twins straddle the split (26.54% of dev rows, 26.37% of sealed-test rows). Every absolute deviance here is optimistic relative to a fresh portfolio; the gap is not, because both tracks consume identical contamination.
  2. The surrogate screen is numeric-only. Region×DrivAge, VehBrand×VehAge and pure threshold interactions are structurally invisible to the ranking and to the E0007/E0008 adoption test. "0 of 10 adopted" bounds what numeric-product translate-back can recover, not what any GLM-representable structure could.
  3. One split, one seed. The paired bootstraps quantify comparison noise within the seed-42 split; they say nothing about split-to-split variation. With no time column available, C1 is an in-period statement, not a forward-in-time generalization.
  4. Hub-prepped data. Pre-clipped, with a silently-binding exposure lower clip — absolute values are not directly comparable to published freMTPL2 numbers from other preparations.
  5. Off-ledger forensics carry no uncertainty. R²_main, the pair rankings, the PD strengths and the M5 shares are same-fold descriptive instruments. They are cited as directional evidence, and every conclusion they support is independently confirmed by a ledger row — no verdict rests on a forensics number alone.
  6. M2-a untested. The CatBoost deficit's attribution was never isolated.

Coding Advice

This is the section that makes the artifact useful. Below is the actual committed train.py — the only mutable surface in a Klein v2 study — followed by the pitfalls that bit this study and the seven pieces of transferable advice they earned.

The winning train.py, verbatim

Read it for its shape first. It is 114 lines and it contains no model code at all: constructors live in the frozen study library pipeline.py, the metric lives in the framework registry, and the per-experiment surface is a nine-line CONFIG block. That is the point — a 5–15 line diff per candidate is only possible if everything else is already immovable.

"""The only per-candidate mutable surface in a Klein v2 study.

freMTPL2 claim frequency, sealed two-track GLM vs GBDT. The CONFIG block is the
experiment surface; pipeline.py is the stable study library. Metric: exposure-
weighted mean Poisson deviance on the scoring fold, computed by the v0.4.0
registry (`kleinlib.eval.evaluate_regression`) — predictions are clipped positive
by the ClippedRegressor wrapper because the registry refuses pred <= 0 by contract.

A sealed final-test run additionally exports the per-row holdout prediction table
(`save_holdout_predictions`) so the off-ledger sealed-gap + paired-SE computation
and the pricing-eval double-lift card can be built after BOTH tracks have spent
their one sealed access.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import os
import time

import math

from kleinlib.eval import evaluate_regression, save_holdout_predictions
from pipeline import effective_trees, fit_model, null_dev_deviance, read_reference

SMOKE = os.environ.get("KLEIN_SMOKE") == "1"
EXPERIMENT_ID = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID") or ("SMOKE" if SMOKE else None)
TRACK = os.environ.get("KLEIN_TRACK") or ("glm" if SMOKE else None)

# ---- CONFIG: the per-experiment surface (keep diffs 5-15 lines) ----
MODEL = "hgbt_ohe"            # glm_ohe | glm_scoped_splines | glm_interactions |
                              # hgbt_ohe | hgbt_monotone | lgbm_poisson | catboost_poisson
SEED = 0
LR = 0.1
MAX_ITER = 200
MAX_LEAF = 31
INTERACTIONS = ()        # e.g. (("BonusMalus", "DrivAge"),) — surrogate-derived only
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

PRED_DIMS = ["DrivAge", "BonusMalus", "VehGas", "Region"]


def main() -> None:
    t0 = time.time()
    evaluation_kind = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND") or (
        "development" if SMOKE else None
    )
    missing = [
        name
        for name, value in (
            ("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND", evaluation_kind),
            ("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID", EXPERIMENT_ID),
            ("KLEIN_TRACK", TRACK),
        )
        if value is None
    ]
    if missing:
        raise RuntimeError(
            "train.py must be invoked through `klein run-one`; missing "
            + ", ".join(missing)
        )
    if evaluation_kind not in {"development", "final_test"}:
        raise RuntimeError(f"invalid KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND={evaluation_kind!r}")

    if evaluation_kind == "development":
        # Split-identity anchor: the null-model development deviance must match
        # prepare.py's reference cell exactly, every run, before any model fits.
        reference = read_reference()["null_dev_deviance"]
        recomputed = null_dev_deviance()
        if abs(reference - recomputed) > 1e-9:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"split-identity anchor FAILED: reference {reference!r} vs "
                f"recomputed {recomputed!r} — split or data drifted, STOP"
            )

    model, X_ev, y_rate_ev, w_ev, fit_seconds, train_n = fit_model(
        MODEL,
        evaluation_kind=evaluation_kind,
        seed=SEED,
        learning_rate=LR,
        max_iter=MAX_ITER,
        max_leaf_nodes=MAX_LEAF,
        interactions=INTERACTIONS,
    )
    evaluate_regression(
        model,
        X_ev,
        y_rate_ev,
        exp_id=EXPERIMENT_ID,
        t0=t0,
        fit_seconds=fit_seconds,
        train_n=train_n,
        val_n=len(X_ev),
        metric_name="val_poisson_deviance",
        metric_goal="lower",
        sample_weight=w_ev,
        study_dir=".",
        extra={"model_config": MODEL, "wall_seconds": time.time() - t0}
        | ({"effective_trees": n_trees} if math.isfinite(n_trees := effective_trees(model)) else {}),
    )

    if evaluation_kind == "final_test" and not SMOKE:
        save_holdout_predictions(
            ".",
            EXPERIMENT_ID,
            y_true=y_rate_ev,
            y_pred=model.predict(X_ev),
            weight=w_ev,
            dims=X_ev[PRED_DIMS],
        )
        print(f"holdout predictions exported: predictions/{EXPERIMENT_ID}_holdout.csv.gz")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Annotated: the five things this file does that matter

  1. The CONFIG block is the entire experiment surface. Seven named configs, a seed, three capacity knobs and an INTERACTIONS tuple. Every one of the twelve ledger rows is a diff inside those nine lines. The comment on INTERACTIONS — "surrogate-derived only" — is the data-card's lookahead directive written where the person editing the file will see it.
  2. It refuses to run outside the harness. KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND, KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID and KLEIN_TRACK must all be present or it raises. A file that can be run by hand is a file whose results can end up unlogged.
  3. The split-identity anchor fires before any model fits. On every development run it recomputes the intercept-only deviance and compares it to the frozen reference cell at 1e−9. If the split, the prep or the weights ever drift, the study stops immediately rather than producing plausible-looking numbers on different data.
  4. wall_seconds is passed explicitly through extra. It looks decorative. It is load-bearing — friction F1 below.
  5. The sealed branch exports per-row predictions. A sealed access is spent once, so it must yield everything downstream analysis will ever need: the joined prediction tables are what the off-ledger sealed-gap and paired-SE computation ran on, and what a pricing-eval double-lift card would consume.

War stories — the traps that actually fired

F1 — a guardrail metric that is written but not printed

E0001 printed the anchor-exact 0.454861 and was still dispositioned discard: "guardrail metric wall_seconds missing". evaluate_regression writes wall_seconds to aux_metrics.tsv, but the runner's guardrail check reads the printed canonical block. One experiment slot, gone.

# the one-line fix that un-burned the slot (E0002's candidate diff):
extra={"model_config": MODEL, "wall_seconds": time.time() - t0}
#                              ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ not decorative

Transferable rule: a declared guardrail must appear in whatever surface the checker actually reads. If your harness reads stdout, write it to stdout.

F2 — per-track state that was derived once, at scaffold time

E0012 was refused: "sealed final-test state is missing for track 'gbdt'". klein new scaffolds a single track, and the initial state derives its final_holdout_access keys from the tracks that existed then. Adding a second track by editing study.yaml — the only way to build a two-track study today — leaves that map stale, and state loading performs no reconciliation.

Transferable rule: any state map derived from a contract must be topped up from the contract at load time, not frozen at creation. Both frictions are filed as framework fixes in section 7 — each one cost a real experiment slot.

The CatBoost prediction-space trap

CatBoostRegressor.predict returns RawFormulaVal — a log — for most losses, and Exponent only for Poisson and Tweedie if you ask. Handing raw scores to a Poisson deviance produces a nonsense number, or gets rejected by the positivity guard, which is the lucky outcome. The call site is frozen in the study library and never inlined in train.py:

class CatBoostPoisson:
    """CatBoost with Poisson loss on the raw frame (native categoricals).
    predict() must return expected counts (exp space), not the raw score."""

    def predict(self, X: pd.DataFrame) -> np.ndarray:
        return np.asarray(self.model.predict(X, prediction_type="Exponent"), dtype=float)

The registry refuses non-positive predictions — by contract

Poisson deviance needs strictly positive μ, and the metric registry raises rather than silently clipping. That is the right contract, but it means every estimator needs a wrapper — which is also the natural place to replay the stateless frame-level transform at predict time, so that fit and predict can never diverge:

class ClippedRegressor:
    def predict(self, X: pd.DataFrame) -> np.ndarray:
        X = _transform_frame(X, shaped=self.shaped, interactions=self.interactions)
        return np.clip(np.asarray(self.inner.predict(X), dtype=float), MIN_PRED, None)

Scoping the spline basis — the study-04 fix, in three lines

Study 04 splined everything the transformer was handed, including the one-hot dummies. The scoped version puts the spline pipeline on the three continuous rating factors only:

("spline", Pipeline([("scale", StandardScaler()),
                     ("spline", SplineTransformer(n_knots=6, degree=3))]), SPLINED),
("num",    StandardScaler(), rest + inter_cols),
("cat",    OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", min_frequency=20), CATEGORICAL),

The punchline from section 5 is worth repeating here: this fix was correct, hygienic, and changed the answer by −0.8 percentage points. Splining a 0/1 column is a no-op in expressiveness. Fix it because it is right, not because you expect it to buy signal.

Monotone constraints index the input columns

The constraint vector is one entry per input feature, categoricals must be 0, and the whole config therefore has to switch to the native-categorical encoder so the index order is the raw NUMERIC + CATEGORICAL order the vector assumes. That is two changes at once, which is exactly why RQ7 needed a same-encoding control:

pre = build_preprocessor(NUMERIC, CATEGORICAL, kind="native")
cst = [1 if c == "BonusMalus" else 0 for c in NUMERIC] + [0] * len(CATEGORICAL)

The effective-capacity probe

Three libraries, three different attributes, one aux column. Without this the RQ2 verdict would have compared 67 trees to 200 and called it "matched capacity":

def effective_trees(clipped) -> float:
    if isinstance(est, HistGradientBoostingRegressor): return float(est.n_iter_)
    if isinstance(inner, CatBoostPoisson):             return float(inner.model.tree_count_)
    if hasattr(est, "booster_"):                       return float(est.booster_.num_trees())
    return float("nan")   # deterministic GLMs: no tree count

The seven transferable rules

  1. [C8] Run the translate-back programme as a screen with a budget, not as a search. Distil the black box on the train fold only into an interpretable surrogate, rank candidate structure by residual explanation, then adopt on development only above the measured floor — and publish screened / adopted either way. Here that was 10 screened, 0 adopted, and the honest zero is a stronger result than any of the ten "improvements" a floor-free search would have reported.
  2. [C9] Report the GAP, never the levels. 26.54% of development rows and 26.37% of sealed-test rows have a feature-identical twin in train, and the three partitions differ in exposure-weighted frequency by ±1.3%. Both effects hit both tracks identically, so the difference survives what the levels do not — as the A/E flip demonstrates, with no model change whatsoever.
  3. [C10] Log EFFECTIVE capacity on every boosted row before you claim a library difference — or a library tie. max_iter / n_estimators / iterations are nominal; n_iter_, booster_.num_trees(), tree_count_ are what you fitted. HGBT early-stops above 10,000 samples by default and delivered 67 of a nominal 200. Match on the effective number and re-run before writing the verdict.
  4. [C11] Measure the paired floor that governs YOUR comparison before believing any gap. There is more than one floor and they differ by a factor of five or more: a GLM fit-seed std of exactly 0 (deterministic solver — quoting a seed floor for a GLM is meaningless), a GBDT fit-seed std of 0.000210, within-track paired SEs of 0.000270 / 0.000287, and a cross-track paired SE of 0.000963 — 4.6× the GBDT seed floor and the only one that legitimately bands the headline gap. Use common random numbers and bootstrap the difference, not the two levels.
  5. [C12] Size-gate the model class before you spend a week on it. Refit both candidates on nested subsamples of your own train fold and plot the gap against n: seven fits, 14 seconds here, and it tells you whether the boosted model is worth its governance overhead at all. Below the crossover a filed additive GLM is not a concession — it is the better model.
  6. [C13] Price a shape constraint against a control that differs ONLY by the constraint, and check the MARGINAL shape first so you know what you are pricing. Differencing against a differently-encoded incumbent measures constraint plus encoding (4.8× floor); the same-encoding control isolates the constraint (3.7×). And confirm the empirical direction before interpreting: a constraint that is true on the margin can still be expensive, because it is the conditional version the library enforces.
