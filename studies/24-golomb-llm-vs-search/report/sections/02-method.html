Intuition: an exam whose answer key is in the textbook

A student who writes the right answer in the first minute has shown you they read the book. A student who gets there after twenty crossed-out attempts may have worked it out, or half-remembered it and fixed the details. The grader is a stencil: it tells right from wrong without knowing how either student got there. Here the answer key is OEIS A003022 (ref:oeis_a003022), the stencil is verify.py, and the two students are an annealer and a language model.

The asymmetry that makes verifier-guided search work is that checking a ruler is cheap and finding a short one is not. Checking takes subtractions and a duplicate test. The tabulated lengths exist because other people ran exhaustive searches, one order at a time (ref:robinson1979, ref:robinson1983, ref:shearer1990). Any proposer may guess; one small checker, written once and never edited by the search, decides.

A pretrained model is not a blind proposer here, because the answers are public. An ML researcher knows this as test-set contamination. So the sharper question is not “does the LLM match more often?” but “when it matches, did it search or remember?” The instrument chosen for that is the timing of a match: a match in the very first batch, before any verifier feedback exists, is what recall predicts.

What is matched and what is not. The budget is matched in candidate evaluations, which are verifier calls. The annealer submits one candidate at a time and sees a verdict on each. The LLM submits 10 per request (200 requests) and sees one text block of 10 verdicts. Wall-clock, tokens, information per step and, above all, each proposer's prior are not matched. The unmatched prior is the confound the study exists to expose.

The two arms

  • Classical search. Start from a random ruler with ends fixed and interior marks drawn at random, aim one length below the best valid ruler, and anneal the interior marks to remove repeated differences (ref:kirkpatrick1983 supplies the acceptance rule). A candidate is a mark list; every call to the private checker spends one evaluation.
  • LLM as proposer of data. In the style of ref:opro2024: the model returns a JSON list of 10 lists of integers, the checker's verdicts on that batch go into the next prompt, and nothing is ever repaired. A missing or unparseable entry still spends its evaluation, so malformed output is not free. The design deliberately differs from FunSearch and AlphaEvolve (ref:funsearch2024, ref:alphaevolve2025), which evolve programs, not objects.

When it pays, when it does not

A matched-budget design on tabulated instances measures recall and search together. It can say “arm X, at budget B, matched the tabulated length on k of 12 pairs”. It cannot say anything new about Golomb rulers, and a miss says nothing about whether a shorter ruler exists. The honest verbs are verified, matched the tabulated length, and did not reach it within 2000 evaluations.
