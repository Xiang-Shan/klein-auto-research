The verdicts

  • The classical search matched 0 of 12 development pairs and 0 of 6 sealed pairs (C1, C5, both confirmed). Its mean gap grew from 5.667 length units at order 8 to 35.333 at order 11, and a re-execution reproduced the count exactly. This describes this annealer at 2000 evaluations only; a published heuristic such as ref:dotu2005 was not run, and a miss here says nothing about whether a match is within reach at another budget.
  • The LLM arm matched 6 of 12 development pairs (C2, exploratory), all three trials of orders 8 and 11 and none of orders 9 and 10. P2 had registered at least 9, so it is refuted. A salted re-query of every call reproduced the count of 6 but not the pair-level detail.
  • The contrast is a count, not a rate (C3, exploratory). The development difference is +6 pairs. The exact McNemar p is 0.03125 if the 12 pairs are independent, which does not clear the Bonferroni level of 0.025 for the two arm contrasts, and 0.5 if the four orders are the unit. The orders, not the pairs, are the honest unit, because the three trials of an order sent byte-identical first requests.
  • On the sealed orders neither arm matched (C5, confirmed): the LLM arm produced no verified ruler at all. The sealed lane was read once per arm, and the sealed cells could not be replicated by design (the engine refuses a second look at the sealed partition), so C1 and C5 rest on development replications that confirm the instrument, not the sealed counts themselves.
  • Predictions: two supported (P1, P4), three refuted (P2, P3, P5), each refutation answered by a dated decision line (C6, a ledger fact).

Surprise 1: the recall signature did not appear

The registered recall test was a match in the first proposal batch, before any verifier feedback. It was seen in 1 of 12 pairs (P3 asked for at least 6). Five of the six matched pairs first reached the tabulated length after feedback, at proposals as late as 74 of 200 (C4).

Surprise 2 (an untested hypothesis): the matches look like retrieval of printed rows

Every sentence in this subsection is a hypothesis of class mechanism-interpretation, strength exploratory. No experiment in this study was designed to test it (C7, C8).

  • Observed. At order 8 every matched ruler is the printed row 0 1 4 9 15 22 32 34. At order 11 every matched ruler is the printed row 0 1 4 13 28 33 47 54 64 70 72. At order 10 all three bests are the first ten marks of that 11-mark row, a valid ruler by inheritance at length 70 against the tabulated 55, and no order-10 proposal ever reached the tabulated 10-mark row.
  • Erratum E1 on C7. The locked sentence calls the tabulated optimum “unique”. That is wrong as stated: at order 8 it is unique only up to reflection, and at order 11 there are two tabulated rulers up to reflection. Across every proposal in the ledger, the only rulers at the tabulated length are the table’s printed rows in their printed orientation; the mirror images and the second 11-mark ruler were never proposed. Identity therefore carries weak, post-hoc information that leans toward retrieval. It is not a test, and C7 stays exploratory.
  • Why it stays a hypothesis. The same facts fit a short local search that converges on a tabulated length. Only a contamination control (an instance with no public answer) could separate the two, and the study ran none.
  • The order-9 hole (C8, also a hypothesis). Order 9 produced no valid ruler in 600 proposals, while orders 10 and 11 did. One reading is that proposals anchor on extending the printed 8-mark row, which usually repeats a difference. But the first nine marks of the printed 11-mark row form a valid 9-mark ruler and were never proposed, so truncating the longer row is not a general strategy of this model.

Surprise 4: the compute behind a “matched” budget

The budget was matched in verifier calls only. What it hid is the cost on the other axes (C10): the LLM arm took roughly four orders of magnitude more wall-clock than the annealer, and carried millions of tokens through the provider.

usd is unrecorded, not zero. The study’s calls block declared no price, so every recorded usd reads 0 (claim C13). The dollar cost of the live model calls is therefore unrecorded; live token counts are recorded, so a price applied later can be computed from them. The call total is lower than the data card’s early estimate because the sealed cell cannot be re-queried by design and the development cell was re-queried once.

What the matched rulers establish, and what they do not

  • A verified ruler of length certifies only the upper bound . The matched rulers give and , both already the OEIS A003022 values (ref:oeis_a003022). The study establishes nothing new about any Golomb length (C16).
  • The unmatched verified rulers give weaker bounds than the table already holds.
  • No lower bound, and no statement that a match is out of reach for any proposer. The LLM arm’s failure to write a valid 12- or 13-mark ruler describes one snapshot under one prompt, not the difficulty of writing one down; explicit constructions exist (ref:singer1938, ref:atkinson1986).
  • One model snapshot. Only qwen-flash-2025-07-28 was queried, at temperature 0.7, as served on the study date. Every LLM statement is about that snapshot.

Controls that exist, and controls that are missing

Present: the exact verifier with a pinned oracle, the table of tabulated lengths, and a ledger cross-check (every pair has exactly 200 calls, and the stored first batch equals the ledger’s first reply in every pair). Missing, by design of the two-arm contract and with both phase allowances spent: a contamination control (an instance with no public answer), a no-feedback LLM arm (the same prompt resampled without verdicts, to separate feedback-steered repair from resampling), a compute-matched or stronger classical arm, a second snapshot or temperature, and an empirical blind-proposal null. Each missing control is the reason a specific claim above is a hypothesis and not a finding.
