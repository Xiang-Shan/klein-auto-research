Next steps + references

What to try next, in priority order

  1. Give each model family its own track. The one question this study could not answer — is the calibrated GLM's rank shortfall real? — needs the gap to be a difference of two sealed numbers, and one track gets one seal. Two tracks declared at the consult gate is the single highest-information change to this design.
  2. Re-measure the floor on a larger development partition. The bar here is 0.0375805 on 5859 rows. Doubling the development partition roughly halves the sampling variance — at the cost of the training-row identity with the earlier study, which is the trade this study made in the other direction and recorded.
  3. Test the duplicate-row question instead of bounding it. Fit on a deduplicated training partition and evaluate on the same development rows. This study bounded the effect at 0.001415 without ever separating “the duplicates do not help” from “these models cannot exploit them”.
  4. Threshold tuning, with a decile-stability measurement. The doctrine is unweighted fitting plus isotonic calibration plus threshold tuning, and only the first two were tested here. The sealed drop of 0.5101 in top-decile lift says the third deserves its own registered measurement rather than an assumption.

What an actuary should carry away

Calibration first: on this book a filable probability is available at a rank cost the measurement cannot resolve. Balanced class weighting should not reach a rate filing — it costs a factor of 4.055 on Brier and buys 0.0390 of a floor. And a fourth decimal of AUC is not a rate change: nothing here is priced, no materiality block is registered, so the honest statement is simply that the registered keep-sized bar was not cleared.
