The question

An actuary has a motor book of 58,592 policies and a claim rate of 0.063968. Someone already modelled it: an earlier study built a three-rung ladder — a logistic GLM on one-hot features, the same GLM with spline terms and isotonic calibration, and a gradient-boosted tree — and wrote six keeps into its ledger, the best at an AUC of 0.664322.

That study had no measured noise floor, no sealed partition and no claims lock. So this one asks the only question worth asking about a result you inherit:

Which of those six keeps survive when the same ladder is re-run under a contract that measures its own resolution first?

It is a port, deliberately. The contract here reproduces the earlier study's training rows exactly — the same 46,873 policies, chosen by index arithmetic before the first gate — and splits its validation set in half: one half to develop on, one half sealed. Every rung is refitted unchanged. So the fitted models are the earlier study's models, and the only thing the port changes about a number is which rows it is graded on.

Three things a reader should take away, and none of them is “use gradient boosting”:

  • A metric without a resolution is a rumour. The keep bar measured here is 0.0375805 of AUC — 0.9671 of the entire spread of the earlier study's six-row ledger.
  • Rank and level are different currencies. The rung that ranks best is calibrated worst by a factor of 3.789, and a technical premium is attached policy by policy, so it lives in the level.
  • An accepted risk that is never measured is an excuse. The data gate here returned NO-GO. What made the override lawful was not the argument; it was the instrument that shipped with it.
