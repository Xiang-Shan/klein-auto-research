12-insurance-claims-frequency

Does the v1 auto-claims quickstart's three-rung model ladder — a raw-feature GLM anchor, a spline + isotonic GLM, and a histogram gradient-boosted tree — reproduce under Klein's schema-3 contract on the same 58,592-policy portfolio, does the insurance doctrine of class_weight=None plus isotonic calibration buy calibration at a rank cost inside the measured floor, and how many of the v1 ledger's keeps survive a keep bar that is measured rather than assumed?

The question

An actuary has a motor book of 58,592 policies and a claim rate of 0.063968. Someone already modelled it: an earlier study built a three-rung ladder — a logistic GLM on one-hot features, the same GLM with spline terms and isotonic calibration, and a gradient-boosted tree — and wrote six keeps into its ledger, the best at an AUC of 0.664322.

That study had no measured noise floor, no sealed partition and no claims lock. So this one asks the only question worth asking about a result you inherit:

Which of those six keeps survive when the same ladder is re-run under a contract that measures its own resolution first?

It is a port, deliberately. The contract here reproduces the earlier study's training rows exactly — the same 46,873 policies, chosen by index arithmetic before the first gate — and splits its validation set in half: one half to develop on, one half sealed. Every rung is refitted unchanged. So the fitted models are the earlier study's models, and the only thing the port changes about a number is which rows it is graded on.

Three things a reader should take away, and none of them is “use gradient boosting”:

  • A metric without a resolution is a rumour. The keep bar measured here is 0.0375805 of AUC — 0.9671 of the entire spread of the earlier study's six-row ledger.
  • Rank and level are different currencies. The rung that ranks best is calibrated worst by a factor of 3.789, and a technical premium is attached policy by policy, so it lives in the level.
  • An accepted risk that is never measured is an excuse. The data gate here returned NO-GO. What made the override lawful was not the argument; it was the instrument that shipped with it.

The method taught

The models here are ordinary. What the study's decisions actually turn on are two measuring instruments, and those are what this section teaches.

Two things can be wrong with a risk score, and AUC sees only one

Pick a random claimant and a random non-claimant. The AUC is the probability the model scores the claimant higher:

That is ranking, and it is all AUC measures. Now take the same model and run every score through any strictly increasing curve — halve them, square them. Concordant pairs stay concordant, so does not move at all, while the probabilities can be off by an order of magnitude. A technical premium is a claim probability multiplied by a severity and attached policy by policy: it lives entirely in that level. A model can rank beautifully and still be unfilable.

Reweighting destroys the level; calibrating repairs it for free

Weighting the 6.4 % of claim rows up until they carry the same total weight as the rest tells the model it lives in a balanced world. For a logit model the whole effect is a shift of the intercept:

The slopes — hence the ranking — barely move, and every fitted probability comes out far too high. Isotonic calibration goes the other way: fit the best non-decreasing staircase from score to observed frequency, which is a constrained maximum-likelihood problem,

solved exactly by pool-adjacent-violators. Because the staircase never goes down it cannot reorder anything, so calibration is free in AUC terms up to the ties it creates and the cross-fitting that keeps it honest. That is the whole of “calibrate, do not reweight”, and this study measures both halves of it on the same rows.

The instrument that decides everything: a paired floor

Every claim in a ladder study has the form “rung B beat rung A by ”. Re-drawing the evaluation rows moves each rung's own AUC by about ±0.017964 here — larger than most of the gaps in the ladder. But both rungs are scored on the same rows, so their errors partly cancel and the difference is better resolved than either level. How much better is an empirical question, and the paired bootstrap answers it: draw one index vector per replicate, apply it to both rungs, and take the spread of the differences.

One draw, not two: two draws would inflate by roughly and quietly raise the bar. The result is the keep bar — the smallest difference this measurement can resolve.

The data story

58,592 motor policies, 45 prepared columns, a claim rate of 0.063968. The preparation is a faithful port: the 2,000-row fixture regenerated from this study's own prepare.py is byte-identical to the fixture the earlier study committed, which is how the port knows it is modelling the same table and not merely a similar one.

The war story that is still live

Seventeen is_* equipment flags plus two more binary categoricals arrive as the strings "Yes" and "No". Under this machine's pandas they report a dtype of str, not object — so a check written as dtype == "object" skips all nineteen and silently drops them from the model. That cost an ancestor campaign about two hours. Every dtype check in this framework is a value-pattern check for exactly that reason, and the clean-room auditor re-confirmed it column by column here.

The gate said NO-GO

A separate auditor, in a fresh context, reading only the contract, the preparation script and the prepared table — never the lab notebook — ran the mechanized four-row leakage audit and failed row 3:

5/6 checks passed: 1 FAIL — any FAIL is a BLOCKER at the DATA gate

Read the last two [OK] lines first: that is the negative control, and it fired correctly. A constant predictor and a label-shuffled predictor both score at chance. A pipeline that cannot score chance at chance cannot be trusted with 0.664051.

Now the failure. 0.051203 of development rows and 0.053242 of sealed rows are byte-identical twins — all 45 columns, target included — of a row in training. The cause is structural: with 22 region codes, 11 vehicle models and 17 binary flags, the rating space is coarse enough that identical rows recur.

Overridden, not fixed — and why that is not the easy way out

Both available fixes (dedupe, or a split grouped on the row-content hash) change which rows are trained on, which destroys the one identity this port is built on and repairs nothing about the number being reproduced. And for a rating model the modelling unit is the cell, not the policy: two policies with identical rating characteristics are supposed to be priced alike, and policy_id — the only identifier of an individual policy — is dropped and crosses nothing.

So the gate was overridden on the record, the card still says NO-GO, and the risk was carried with a number attached. Every run from that point prints its AUC restricted to the evaluation rows with no twin among the rows it was fitted on. The measured answer, four rungs later: at most 0.001415 of AUC, and three of the four development rungs score higher without the twins. The same table also measured the earlier study's own validation partition — 0.052223 of it carried a training twin, and nobody had ever looked.

The experiment journey

Phase 0: measure the instrument before using it

Three recipes, three estimands, and they disagree by four orders of magnitude. That spread is the whole reason the framework refuses to let a study guess its keep bar.

  • Fit noise (five fit seeds, same rows): standard deviation 2.50998e-06. Recorded as provenance and never as the bar — a study that pasted this in would keep anything that moved the fifth decimal.
  • Marginal re-split (ten re-draws inside the train+development pool, never touching the seal): standard deviation 0.017964. How much one candidate's own score moves when the rows change.
  • Paired comparison (the bar): the declared pair, its replicate count and its rationale all written into the notebook before the measurement. Standard deviation 0.0173021, range 0.075161, so gives 0.0375805.

That bar is 0.9671 of the distance from the earlier study's worst recorded model to its best. Before a single candidate ran, the ladder was already known to be almost entirely inside its own measurement error.

Read the ledger with the floor in hand:

  • E0001 is the positive control: it had to recover a number an independent study recorded on the identical training rows, and did, to 0.011322 — almost exactly the 0.011226 the closed-form variance predicted for a transfer of this shape. Had it missed, the plan said to stop rather than absorb it into the ladder.
  • E0002 reproduces the spline-and-isotonic rung to 0.001612 and then discards: its whole paired AUC lift over the anchor is 0.9568 of one floor. Feature engineering plus calibration, on this book, is worth slightly less than the smallest difference the measurement can see.
  • E0003 reproduces the tree rung to 0.001154 and keeps, +0.049911 over the incumbent — the study's one frontier improvement. Its edge over the calibrated GLM, though, is 0.3714 of a floor.
  • E0004 is the doctrine A/B and it discards on AUC while winning decisively on level. That is the point of the run, not a disappointment.
  • E0005 spends the one sealed access on the incumbent, unchanged, after the mandatory dry-run rehearsal.

The picture the study is really about

Every gap the ladder measured, drawn twice: in metric units against the declared bar, and divided by the floor that actually prices each comparison. Both panels are zero-based.

The right panel is where the honesty lives. The spline rung sits at 0.9568 of the declared bar — refuted — and at 1.0926 of the floor of the comparison it actually makes, where it would have been supported. The registered verdict stands, because a bar chosen after the measurement is not a bar; but that refutation is instrument-limited, and the study had pre-committed to saying so.

Then the seal

Fitted on train plus development, evaluated once on the sealed half: 0.657739 against a development score of 0.664051 — a shift of 0.1680 of one floor. The level held. What did not hold was the top-decile lift, which fell 0.5101, from 2.2167 to 1.7067, at a claim rate of 0.063968: a decile rests on a tenth of the rows, and it is a far noisier statistic than the AUC computed beside it.

Finally klein replicate re-executed the kept run in a detached worktree and reproduced it exactly, difference 0.

Findings & insights

The ladder reproduced; the keeps did not

All three rungs land inside the tolerance that was derived before any fit — residuals of 0.011322, 0.001612 and 0.001154 against 0.0225. The models port. What does not port is the verdict: under a measured bar the same ladder yields exactly one frontier improvement instead of six keeps, and the earlier study's sixth row — a learning-rate sweep recorded as a keep on an AUC lift of 0.001425 — is 0.0379 of one floor. Under a measured bar it is a discard.

Prose that names its kwargs is nearly as good as a commit

One rung was recoverable verbatim from a committed file; two survived only as prose. But that prose named its three non-default constructor arguments, because the earlier study had made naming them its own parting advice. The verbatim rung reproduces to 0.001154 and the prose rung to 0.001612: the whole advantage of having the code is 0.000458 of AUC. That is a stronger endorsement of the advice than the prior that predicted it.

Calibration is cheap and enormous; rank is expensive and invisible

Replacing balanced class weighting with unweighted fitting plus a cross-fitted isotonic wrapper improves the Brier score by a factor of 4.055, from 0.240641 to 0.059337, and pays 0.001465 of AUC — 0.0390 of one floor, one twenty-fifth of the smallest difference this study can resolve. Meanwhile the tree that ranks best carries a Brier score worse by a factor of 3.789 than the calibrated GLM, and its rank advantage over that GLM is 0.3714 of a floor. Rank and level are not two views of one quantity; they are two currencies, and the exchange rate here is brutal.

The profile's rank views of the same incumbent, for completeness: the ROC curve the primary metric integrates, and the precision-recall curve, which is the harder read at a claim rate of 0.063968 and the one that shows how little of the book any model can pick out with confidence.

Four surprises, each with a mechanism

  • Pairing barely helped. The paired spread between the GLM and the tree is 0.013942 against a marginal spread of 0.017964. Cancellation needs the two scores to move together, and these two models rank these policies too differently. Two rungs differing by one lever pair down to 0.001276 instead — a ratio of 10.92 on the same rows with the same instrument.
  • A verdict flipped between two defensible floors, and the study had pre-committed to reporting it. Exactly one registered verdict in this study is instrument-limited, and it says so.
  • The duplicated rows flattered nobody. The prior was that a tree could memorise a duplicated rating cell and a GLM could not. It did not: three of four development rungs score higher on the twin-free rows, and the largest gap in either direction is 0.001415. A coarse cell is what all of these models price as a group anyway — there is nothing individual left in the row to memorise. The confirmed claim was checked the same way: on the 5528 sealed rows with no training twin the incumbent scores 0.656647, a gap of 0.001091.
  • A rung reproduced while failing to converge. The spline fits hit the solver's iteration cap; the plain anchor does not. The rung still lands 0.001612 from its target, which means the original fit was equally unconverged. What reproduced was an implementation, not an ideal — and a study that “fixed” the convergence would have missed the anchor it was chasing.

What the lock says

Twelve claims across four classes. Exactly one reaches confirmed — the incumbent's level on the sealed partition — because one track gets one sealed access, and that confirms a level, never a difference. Every rung-to-rung gap on this page is development evidence and is exploratory by construction, which the plan registered before the first run rather than discovering afterwards.

Model coding advice

Here is the entire per-candidate surface of this study — the actual bytes, included by reference so this page cannot drift from the code that produced the numbers above. Read the four constants under “the candidate” first. That block is an experiment; everything else is machinery that a per-experiment edit is not allowed to touch.

"""The only per-candidate mutable surface in this study.

The per-experiment diff is the four constants under "the candidate" — ``CANDIDATE``
(the rung being tried), ``REFERENCE`` (the rung it claims to beat, refitted on the same
rows in this same process), ``V1_ANCHOR`` (the value the v1 ledger recorded for this
rung, or ``None``) and ``DEV_INCUMBENT`` (set only for the sealed run) — and nothing
else. The rung definitions live in ``lib/rungs.py``, which is stable study library code:
a per-experiment edit must not be able to change what Phase 0 measured its floor on.

What every run prints, on top of the canonical block
---------------------------------------------------
Nothing here is a literal a later reader must trust: the floor comes from the contract,
the anchor comes from the v1 ledger transcribed in ``scouting_ledger.md``, and the
reference rung is refitted on the SAME evaluation rows so a comparison lives inside one
printed block instead of being subtracted across two log files.

============================ =================================================
``rung``                     which rung this run is
``v1_anchor``                what the v1 study recorded for this rung, on its
                             own (two-way) partition
``anchor_gap``               ``val_auc - v1_anchor`` — the transfer residual
``reference_rung``           the rung refitted here for comparison
``reference_auc``            that rung's AUC on these same rows
``delta_vs_reference``       ``val_auc - reference_auc`` (a PAIRED difference)
``delta_in_floors``          that difference in units of the contract's
                             ``minimum_delta`` — the number P3, P5 and P6 read
``reference_brier``          the reference rung's Brier score on these rows
``brier_delta_vs_reference`` candidate Brier minus reference Brier; negative
                             means better-calibrated
``dev_incumbent``            (sealed run only) the development incumbent's score
``sealed_shift_in_floors``   (sealed run only) the sealed score's distance from
                             that incumbent, in floors
``twin_free_rows``           evaluation rows with NO byte-identical twin among the
                             rows this run was fitted on
``twin_free_auc``            the AUC on exactly those rows
``twin_free_gap``            ``twin_free_auc - val_auc`` — how much of the headline
                             number the duplicated rows were worth
============================ =================================================

The last three exist because the DATA gate FAILED this study on duplicate row content
straddling the partitions and the gate was overridden rather than fixed (`program.md`,
2026-09-03; `data_card.md` BLOCKER #1). An accepted risk that is never measured is an
excuse, so every run measures it. ``primary_metric`` deliberately stays the
full-partition AUC: the registered anchors compare against v1 values computed the same
contaminated way, and re-defining the measurement would make that comparison
meaningless.

``delta_in_floors`` and ``sealed_shift_in_floors`` are printed only once the contract
carries a measured ``minimum_delta``. Before Phase 0 sets it there is no floor to divide
by, and a prediction whose key is not printed is INCONCLUSIVE rather than refuted, which
is the honest answer.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import os
import time

from sklearn.metrics import brier_score_loss, roc_auc_score

import kleinlib
from kleinlib.contract import load_contract
from kleinlib.data import load_partition
from lib.duplicate_exposure import duplicate_free_mask
from lib.rungs import fit_rung, positive_probabilities

SMOKE = os.environ.get("KLEIN_SMOKE") == "1"
EXPERIMENT_ID = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID") or ("SMOKE" if SMOKE else None)
TRACK = os.environ.get("KLEIN_TRACK") or ("primary" if SMOKE else None)

# --- the candidate: the whole per-experiment diff surface -------------------
# Empty between experiments. Choosing a rung IS the falsifiable idea a candidate
# transaction carries, so the surface carries no rung until one is chosen.
CANDIDATE = "hgbt_balanced"          # E0003: the v1 tree rung, recoverable verbatim
REFERENCE = "glm_splines_isotonic"   # the calibrated GLM rung it claims to beat
V1_ANCHOR = 0.662897                 # scouting_ledger.md S1, v1 results.tsv row 3
DEV_INCUMBENT = None             # set only for the sealed confirmation run


def main() -> None:
    t0 = time.time()
    evaluation_kind = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND")
    if SMOKE:
        evaluation_kind = evaluation_kind or "development"
    missing = [
        name
        for name, value in (
            ("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND", evaluation_kind),
            ("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID", EXPERIMENT_ID),
            ("KLEIN_TRACK", TRACK),
        )
        if value is None
    ]
    if missing:
        raise RuntimeError(
            "train.py must be invoked through `klein run-one`. For a pre-run "
            "syntax/shape check use `KLEIN_SMOKE=1 python train.py` — it prints the "
            "canonical block, writes no sidecars or snapshots, and is not evidence. "
            "Missing: " + ", ".join(missing)
        )

    if CANDIDATE is None:
        raise RuntimeError(
            "no rung is chosen: set CANDIDATE to a key of lib.rungs.RECIPES. The "
            "surface is empty between experiments on purpose — choosing the rung is "
            "the one falsifiable idea a candidate transaction carries."
        )

    X_fit, X_eval, y_fit, y_eval = load_partition(evaluation_kind, study_dir=".")
    minimum_delta = float(
        load_contract(".")["tracks"][TRACK]["metric"].get("minimum_delta") or 0.0
    )

    fit_start = time.time()
    model, _, X_eval_t = fit_rung(CANDIDATE, X_fit, X_eval, y_fit)
    fit_seconds = time.time() - fit_start

    probabilities = positive_probabilities(model, X_eval_t)
    candidate_auc = float(roc_auc_score(y_eval, probabilities))
    candidate_brier = float(brier_score_loss(y_eval, probabilities))

    # The overridden DATA-gate BLOCKER, measured on this run's own rows.
    twin_free = duplicate_free_mask(X_fit, y_fit, X_eval, y_eval)
    twin_free_auc = float(roc_auc_score(y_eval[twin_free], probabilities[twin_free]))

    extra: dict[str, str] = {
        "rung": CANDIDATE,
        "twin_free_rows": str(int(twin_free.sum())),
        "twin_free_auc": f"{twin_free_auc:.6f}",
        "twin_free_gap": f"{twin_free_auc - candidate_auc:.6f}",
    }
    if V1_ANCHOR is not None:
        extra["v1_anchor"] = f"{V1_ANCHOR:.6f}"
        extra["anchor_gap"] = f"{candidate_auc - V1_ANCHOR:.6f}"
    if REFERENCE is not None:
        reference_model, _, X_reference_eval = fit_rung(REFERENCE, X_fit, X_eval, y_fit)
        reference_probabilities = positive_probabilities(reference_model, X_reference_eval)
        reference_auc = float(roc_auc_score(y_eval, reference_probabilities))
        reference_brier = float(brier_score_loss(y_eval, reference_probabilities))
        extra["reference_rung"] = REFERENCE
        extra["reference_auc"] = f"{reference_auc:.6f}"
        extra["delta_vs_reference"] = f"{candidate_auc - reference_auc:.6f}"
        extra["reference_brier"] = f"{reference_brier:.6f}"
        extra["brier_delta_vs_reference"] = f"{candidate_brier - reference_brier:.6f}"
        if minimum_delta > 0:
            extra["delta_in_floors"] = f"{(candidate_auc - reference_auc) / minimum_delta:.4f}"
    if DEV_INCUMBENT is not None:
        extra["dev_incumbent"] = f"{DEV_INCUMBENT:.6f}"
        if minimum_delta > 0:
            extra["sealed_shift_in_floors"] = (
                f"{(candidate_auc - DEV_INCUMBENT) / minimum_delta:.4f}"
            )

    kleinlib.eval.evaluate(
        model,
        X_eval_t,
        y_eval,
        exp_id=EXPERIMENT_ID,
        study_dir=".",
        t0=t0,
        fit_seconds=fit_seconds,
        train_n=len(X_fit),
        val_n=len(X_eval),
        metric_name="val_auc",
        metric_goal="higher",
        extra=extra,
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Six things worth stealing

  • Keep the rung definitions out of the mutable surface. They live in lib/rungs.py, which both the entrypoint and the Phase 0 floor script import. Re-typing them in the floor script would let the floor describe a model the ledger never ran; importing them from the entrypoint would let a per-experiment edit silently change what the floor was measured on. A stable library has neither failure, and the per-experiment diff shrinks to four lines.
  • Leave the candidate slot empty between experiments. CANDIDATE = None and a main that refuses to run without a rung. Choosing the rung is the one falsifiable idea a candidate carries, so the surface should not silently still hold the last one.
  • Read the bar from the contract; never write it as a literal. minimum_delta is loaded from study.yaml inside main. Before Phase 0 sets it there is no floor to divide by, so the floor-relative keys are simply not printed — and a prediction whose key is missing is inconclusive, not refuted, which is the honest answer.
  • Route every partition through load_partition. It reads the contract alone and prints the split_fingerprint: line the notary compares against the one the data gate froze, so a partition that silently changed is a crash by design. The only integer seed in this study's code is a fit seed, and it reaches nothing but two estimator constructors.
  • Print the comparison; do not reconstruct it. Every run refits its named reference rung on the same rows, in the same process, and prints delta_vs_reference, delta_in_floors and brier_delta_vs_reference. A paired comparison assembled later by subtracting two log files is not paired; it is two numbers that happen to be near each other.
  • Ship an instrument with every accepted risk. The three twin_free_* keys are four lines of entrypoint. They are the difference between “we accepted a known leakage risk” and “the accepted risk is worth at most 0.001415 of AUC per rung, and here is the number on every run, including the sealed one”.

Pitfalls this study actually hit

  • A floor is a property of the pair, not of the data. Measure the floor of the comparison you are going to make, and if you declare one global bar on a ladder that spans model classes, say so and publish the pair-specific numbers beside it.
  • The order statistic in the bar rule grows with the replicate count. is a guard against underestimating from few replicates; at a thousand replicates it inflates the bar for a reason that is arithmetic, not measurement. Choose the replicate count deliberately, write the choice down first, and report what the other choice would have given.
  • A claims lock is not a draft. A number's home may never change, and a claim's sentence may never change. Home magnitudes where magnitudes live, and keep identifiers containing digits out of claim sentences — the scanner reads the digit in log1p as a number nobody measured.

Next steps + references

What to try next, in priority order

  1. Give each model family its own track. The one question this study could not answer — is the calibrated GLM's rank shortfall real? — needs the gap to be a difference of two sealed numbers, and one track gets one seal. Two tracks declared at the consult gate is the single highest-information change to this design.
  2. Re-measure the floor on a larger development partition. The bar here is 0.0375805 on 5859 rows. Doubling the development partition roughly halves the sampling variance — at the cost of the training-row identity with the earlier study, which is the trade this study made in the other direction and recorded.
  3. Test the duplicate-row question instead of bounding it. Fit on a deduplicated training partition and evaluate on the same development rows. This study bounded the effect at 0.001415 without ever separating “the duplicates do not help” from “these models cannot exploit them”.
  4. Threshold tuning, with a decile-stability measurement. The doctrine is unweighted fitting plus isotonic calibration plus threshold tuning, and only the first two were tested here. The sealed drop of 0.5101 in top-decile lift says the third deserves its own registered measurement rather than an assumption.

What an actuary should carry away

Calibration first: on this book a filable probability is available at a rank cost the measurement cannot resolve. Balanced class weighting should not reach a rate filing — it costs a factor of 4.055 on Brier and buys 0.0390 of a floor. And a fourth decimal of AUC is not a rate change: nothing here is priced, no materiality block is registered, so the honest statement is simply that the registered keep-sized bar was not cleared.
