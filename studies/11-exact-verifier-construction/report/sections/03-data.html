3 · The data story

This study has no dataset. Its data.modality is none, and Klein's DATA gate is typed by modality: where a tabular study gets a profile and a four-row leakage audit, a verifier-only study gets a verifier card. The audit is of the oracle, because the oracle is the evidence source.

§3.1 · What the gate actually froze

prepare.py writes a problem statement, not data, and the DATA gate hashes it. Inside data/prepared/instances.json:

  • the two instances — 11 × 11 and 31 × 31 — each with its pigeonhole bound and the argument for it in words;
  • the two seed blocks: development, the only block adaptive work may use, and sealed, a block no development run may touch;
  • the registered budget ladder: 20000, 200000, 2000000 addability tests;
  • the verifier's two controls.

The seed blocks are how a study with no rows gets a sealed partition. data.split.kind is none, so there is nothing to partition; klein run-one --final-test sets KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND=final_test and search.py reads the sealed block by name out of the hashed file. No literal seed appears in search.py, verify.py or lib/ — war story 8's failure mode (an evaluator carrying a retired split seed) cannot occur when there is no split to carry.

§3.2 · The controls, and why freezing them first is the whole trick

The naming follows the protocol's detector convention: the positive control is the one on which the detector must fire; the negative control is the one on which it must stay silent.

Positive — twelve planted invalid objects the checker must reject. Seven geometric defects spanning the slopes 0, undefined, +1, -1, +2, +1/2 and a +2 line with unequal lattice gaps; three well-formedness defects (a coordinate equal to , a negative coordinate, a non-integer coordinate); a repeated lattice point; and one almost valid object — the parabola set plus a single cell that creates exactly one collinear triple among its twelve points, which a checker that sampled triples would probably pass.

Negative — one known-valid object the checker must accept, at a value known in advance. The Erdős parabola set on the 11 × 11 grid: valid by the proof in §2.5, so its score is 11 before any code runs.

A positive control that can be softened after it fails is not a control. These twelve objects were hashed into the DATA gate record before the checker ever met them, and the gate's sha256 is on events.jsonl.

§3.3 · The go/no-go call

The card ruled GO, with the checker exercised in both directions before any search existed: it fired on all twelve planted objects and accepted the known-valid one at exactly its predicted 11. That exercise was the audit, not the evidence — it ran outside a transaction, so E0001 re-ran the same battery under the notary and that is where the prediction was adjudicated.

The card's ranked issues were four NOTEs and one WARN, and the WARN is the honest one: the search and the checker share an idea — both decide collinearity — even though they share no code, so a common conceptual error would survive their agreement. The seven planted geometric controls exist to span exactly that failure class, and the checker uses the general cross product, which has no special cases at all.

§3.4 · One correction, filed after the fact

The card also wrote, at the gate and before any run existed, that the checker's cost would be "sub-millisecond at every size this study uses". That was a fair pre-run estimate and it is wrong: the verifier logs record 0.002046 seconds on the largest object, and four of the ten exceed a millisecond. The card is a gate-hashed artifact and is not edited — editing it after the runs would silently invalidate the record it is part of — so the correction lives in findings.md, in program.md, and in erratum E1 against claim C6. §5 tells that story properly; it is the study's own best example of why a number needs a home.
