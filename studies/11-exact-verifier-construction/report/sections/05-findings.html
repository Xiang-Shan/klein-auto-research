§5.3 · The surprise, and what it does not license

P1 was written expecting the maximum at n = 11 and got 21. P6 was written expecting the same thing from a block nobody had touched, and got 22. The development trajectory found its best at evaluation 152572 and then spent the rest of 2000000 without improving, so "more budget" was demonstrably not the missing ingredient — the honest reading of the development lane alone ("this search plateaus at 21 here") was a statement about one draw wearing the costume of a statement about the search.

A difference, not a rate. Two seeds, one success. This study cannot say how often the search reaches 22, and does not. A registered seed sweep is the first item in §7 for exactly that reason.

P1 stays refuted and is not re-scoped by P6. Two predictions, two lanes, two verdicts — rewriting the first in the light of the second would delete the most informative thing the study measured.

§5.4 · Consequences for the bound

What the objects establish: a lower bound. The 22-point object attains at ; the 55-point object is a lower bound on what this search reaches at and nothing more.

What they do not establish: nothing about the problem. The 21-point plateau and the 54/55-point results are search limits — facts about one algorithm under one budget from two named seeds — and the correct reading of every one of them is "did not reach", never "cannot". The study can say that with unusual confidence because the counterexample is inside it: the same search missed 22 from one seed and reached it from another. No upper bound is established or improved; the only one in play, , was a theorem before the study began, which is precisely what headroom zero means.

§5.5 · The erratum, and why it is in the tutorial

The referee found that C6's locked sentence ends "and stayed sub-millisecond", and that C6's own pinned artifact refutes it: runs/E0010/verify.log, the file that supplies C6's 26235, records 0.002046 seconds beside it. Four of the ten verifier logs exceed a millisecond; sub-millisecond holds only for the 11 × 11 objects, where E0009 reads 0.000169.

The phrase came from the data card, which wrote it at Gate 1 as a pre-run cost estimate — before any run existed — and it was never re-checked once the logs existed. So: erratum E1 is filed against C6, the two measured seconds are now pinned numbers attached to it, and the findings and the knowledge entry quote them. C6's sentence itself is untouched, because claims.lock is append-only and an erratum re-scopes rather than rewrites — a reader sees both what was claimed and what was later learned.

Why it survived every mechanical check: "sub-millisecond" is a word, not a numeral, so klein verify --numbers was never looking at it. A numeral scan catches invented numbers; it does not catch an adjective that has quietly become a claim.

§5.6 · Why this study closes exploratory

Not a shortfall — arithmetic. confirmation.require is [verify], which on a frontier track resolves to a re-verification record for the track's final keep. There are no keeps and there could not have been, so there is no incumbent to confirm and the engine labels the study exploratory. The label does not mean the objects are unverified: every development run carries a re-verification at tolerance 0, both procedural-verdict claims close confirmed, and the referee re-ran the declared verifier itself, in a fresh process, on the pinned object — and got 22.
