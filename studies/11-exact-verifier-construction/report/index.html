1 · The question

Put as many points as you can on the grid of lattice points so that no straight line passes through three of them — any line, not just rows, columns and diagonals. Dudeney posed it in 1900 with sixteen pawns on a chessboard.

The ceiling is free. Three points in one row are collinear, so each row holds at most two, and there are rows:

That is a one-line argument, and it is a theorem: no configuration found by any method can exceed it. The floor is not free — whether is actually attained at a given is a hard question, and past the small cases the answers come from computer search.

So this study asks a question with two halves, and the second half is the one it exists for:

On an integer grid, how many points can a budgeted iterated local search place with no three collinear — judged only by a declared exact verifier that shares no code with the search — at an where the proven maximum should be reachable and at an where it should not?

Why an optimize study, and why this problem

A search that scores itself is a witness testifying about its own case. Klein's optimize contract breaks that mechanically: the track declares a verifier, the verifier lives outside the per-experiment mutable surface, the METHOD gate hashes it so it can never change again, and klein run-one runs it as a second process on the artifact the search wrote. Its number is the one that enters the ledger. The searcher's own claim is recorded beside it, and a disagreement is a crash.

No-three-in-line is the right problem to demonstrate that on, for one reason: the objective is an integer — how many points are in the set — so the checker needs no tolerance. Two integers either agree or they do not.

2 · The method taught

Two pieces of code do all the work here, and the whole point of the study is that they must not be the same piece. Learn the checker first — in an optimize study the checker is the load-bearing part, and Klein's METHOD gate treats it that way: the Practice leg of the Theory + Papers + Practice triad is the from-scratch verifier, not the searcher.

§2.1 · The one equation

Three points with integer coordinates are collinear exactly when the cross product of the two edge vectors vanishes:

The quantity is twice the signed area of the triangle. It is an integer, so the test is exact: there is no division, no slope, no floating point and no rounding decision anywhere on the path. A configuration is valid when that expression is non-zero for every triple it contains.

§2.2 · The checker: enumerate everything

The verifier is deliberately the dumbest correct implementation. It reads the artifact, checks it is well-formed — integer coordinates (a Python bool is an int, and is refused explicitly), on the grid, no repeats — and then walks every triple:

for a, b, c in combinations(points, 3):
    cross = (b[0]-a[0])*(c[1]-a[1]) - (b[1]-a[1])*(c[0]-a[0])
    if cross == 0:
        raise Rejected(f"points {a}, {b} and {c} are collinear")
return len(points)

No sampling, no early exit that could hide a triple, no clever geometry. On the largest object this study verified that is 26235 cross products, and the checker's own log records 0.002046 seconds for it — cheap, and the cost is charged to no guardrail.

§2.3 · The searcher: hash directions instead

The search may be as clever as it likes. Adding a point to a valid set can only create triples that contain , and is collinear exactly when is parallel to . So look at the world from : reduce each offset to its primitive direction and ask whether two of them collide.

That turns an question into an one — and it is not what the checker does. Two deliberately different algorithms agreeing is evidence; one algorithm agreeing with itself is a tautology.

§2.4 · The search loop

Iterated local search over configurations that are valid at every instant: complete greedily, keep improvements, perturb, repeat.

S = []                                  # always a VALID configuration
while evaluations < B:
    order = shuffle(all cells); order.sort(key=lambda c: rows[c.y] + cols[c.x])
    for p in order:                     # one pass = one greedy completion
        evaluations += 1                # ONE EVALUATION = one addability test
        if addable(p, S): S.append(p)
    if len(S) > len(best): best = S[:]  # accept improvements only
    elif stale >= 2n:     S = []        # restart from empty
    else:                 remove 1-3 random points from S

Two details matter. The budget unit is one call to addable — the atomic cost of the search — and it is what the registered guardrail caps. The ordering is most-constrained-first: a configuration needs exactly two points in every row and every column, so the emptiest lines are the ones still to be filled; the shuffle comes before the sort, so ties break randomly and every pass is a fresh sample.

The whole thing is deterministic in : one random stream drives every choice and the budget only says when to stop. A larger budget is therefore a strict extension of a smaller one, which is why the ladder below is one trajectory read at three points and its objective is monotone by construction rather than by luck.

3 · The data story

This study has no dataset. Its data.modality is none, and Klein's DATA gate is typed by modality: where a tabular study gets a profile and a four-row leakage audit, a verifier-only study gets a verifier card. The audit is of the oracle, because the oracle is the evidence source.

§3.1 · What the gate actually froze

prepare.py writes a problem statement, not data, and the DATA gate hashes it. Inside data/prepared/instances.json:

  • the two instances — 11 × 11 and 31 × 31 — each with its pigeonhole bound and the argument for it in words;
  • the two seed blocks: development, the only block adaptive work may use, and sealed, a block no development run may touch;
  • the registered budget ladder: 20000, 200000, 2000000 addability tests;
  • the verifier's two controls.

The seed blocks are how a study with no rows gets a sealed partition. data.split.kind is none, so there is nothing to partition; klein run-one --final-test sets KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND=final_test and search.py reads the sealed block by name out of the hashed file. No literal seed appears in search.py, verify.py or lib/ — war story 8's failure mode (an evaluator carrying a retired split seed) cannot occur when there is no split to carry.

§3.2 · The controls, and why freezing them first is the whole trick

The naming follows the protocol's detector convention: the positive control is the one on which the detector must fire; the negative control is the one on which it must stay silent.

Positive — twelve planted invalid objects the checker must reject. Seven geometric defects spanning the slopes 0, undefined, +1, -1, +2, +1/2 and a +2 line with unequal lattice gaps; three well-formedness defects (a coordinate equal to , a negative coordinate, a non-integer coordinate); a repeated lattice point; and one almost valid object — the parabola set plus a single cell that creates exactly one collinear triple among its twelve points, which a checker that sampled triples would probably pass.

Negative — one known-valid object the checker must accept, at a value known in advance. The Erdős parabola set on the 11 × 11 grid: valid by the proof in §2.5, so its score is 11 before any code runs.

A positive control that can be softened after it fails is not a control. These twelve objects were hashed into the DATA gate record before the checker ever met them, and the gate's sha256 is on events.jsonl.

§3.3 · The go/no-go call

The card ruled GO, with the checker exercised in both directions before any search existed: it fired on all twelve planted objects and accepted the known-valid one at exactly its predicted 11. That exercise was the audit, not the evidence — it ran outside a transaction, so E0001 re-ran the same battery under the notary and that is where the prediction was adjudicated.

The card's ranked issues were four NOTEs and one WARN, and the WARN is the honest one: the search and the checker share an idea — both decide collinearity — even though they share no code, so a common conceptual error would survive their agreement. The seven planted geometric controls exist to span exactly that failure class, and the checker uses the general cross product, which has no special cases at all.

§3.4 · One correction, filed after the fact

The card also wrote, at the gate and before any run existed, that the checker's cost would be "sub-millisecond at every size this study uses". That was a fair pre-run estimate and it is wrong: the verifier logs record 0.002046 seconds on the largest object, and four of the ten exceed a millisecond. The card is a gate-hashed artifact and is not edited — editing it after the runs would silently invalidate the record it is part of — so the correction lives in findings.md, in program.md, and in erratum E1 against claim C6. §5 tells that story properly; it is the study's own best example of why a number needs a home.

§4.5 · E0009, E0010 — the sealed cells, and the surprise

Each track gets one sealed access, rehearsed first with klein run-one --final-test --dry-run (which hands back the development block and prints sealed_dryrun: 1, so the seal survives the rehearsal). The sealed cells then run the same search at the same largest budget from the sealed seed block — one integer different, nothing else.

At n = 31, 55 — a little better than the development lane's 54, still far short. P7 supported.

At n = 11, 22 — the proven maximum, found at evaluation 1612132. P6 supported, and P1's refutation stands beside it, unrewritten.

Same instance, same algorithm, same budget, same commit; the two lanes land on opposite sides of a theorem's ceiling. That pair is the finding, and §5 says what may and may not be concluded from it.

§4.6 · Re-verification

Every development run was re-checked with klein replicate --verify-only: the declared verifier re-run in a fresh process on the pinned artifact, no search, no worktree. Seven records, reproduced: true in every one, tolerance 0.0 taken from verifier.tolerance, difference 0.0 on the primary metric. P4 supported.

§5.3 · The surprise, and what it does not license

P1 was written expecting the maximum at n = 11 and got 21. P6 was written expecting the same thing from a block nobody had touched, and got 22. The development trajectory found its best at evaluation 152572 and then spent the rest of 2000000 without improving, so "more budget" was demonstrably not the missing ingredient — the honest reading of the development lane alone ("this search plateaus at 21 here") was a statement about one draw wearing the costume of a statement about the search.

A difference, not a rate. Two seeds, one success. This study cannot say how often the search reaches 22, and does not. A registered seed sweep is the first item in §7 for exactly that reason.

P1 stays refuted and is not re-scoped by P6. Two predictions, two lanes, two verdicts — rewriting the first in the light of the second would delete the most informative thing the study measured.

§5.4 · Consequences for the bound

What the objects establish: a lower bound. The 22-point object attains at ; the 55-point object is a lower bound on what this search reaches at and nothing more.

What they do not establish: nothing about the problem. The 21-point plateau and the 54/55-point results are search limits — facts about one algorithm under one budget from two named seeds — and the correct reading of every one of them is "did not reach", never "cannot". The study can say that with unusual confidence because the counterexample is inside it: the same search missed 22 from one seed and reached it from another. No upper bound is established or improved; the only one in play, , was a theorem before the study began, which is precisely what headroom zero means.

§5.5 · The erratum, and why it is in the tutorial

The referee found that C6's locked sentence ends "and stayed sub-millisecond", and that C6's own pinned artifact refutes it: runs/E0010/verify.log, the file that supplies C6's 26235, records 0.002046 seconds beside it. Four of the ten verifier logs exceed a millisecond; sub-millisecond holds only for the 11 × 11 objects, where E0009 reads 0.000169.

The phrase came from the data card, which wrote it at Gate 1 as a pre-run cost estimate — before any run existed — and it was never re-checked once the logs existed. So: erratum E1 is filed against C6, the two measured seconds are now pinned numbers attached to it, and the findings and the knowledge entry quote them. C6's sentence itself is untouched, because claims.lock is append-only and an erratum re-scopes rather than rewrites — a reader sees both what was claimed and what was later learned.

Why it survived every mechanical check: "sub-millisecond" is a word, not a numeral, so klein verify --numbers was never looking at it. A numeral scan catches invented numbers; it does not catch an adjective that has quietly become a claim.

§5.6 · Why this study closes exploratory

Not a shortfall — arithmetic. confirmation.require is [verify], which on a frontier track resolves to a re-verification record for the track's final keep. There are no keeps and there could not have been, so there is no incumbent to confirm and the engine labels the study exploratory. The label does not mean the objects are unverified: every development run carries a re-verification at tolerance 0, both procedural-verdict claims close confirmed, and the referee re-ran the declared verifier itself, in a fresh process, on the pinned object — and got 22.

6 · Search and verifier coding advice

Four lessons, each with the run that paid for it. They are the study's research-discipline claims, and they are what transfers.

§6.1 · Write the checker as the dumbest correct thing — C6

Here is the declared verifier, in full, as the bytes the METHOD gate hashed and the notary ran ten times. Read the check function: it is a sequence of refusals, then a loop over every triple.

"""The declared verifier — the checker, never the searcher.

`klein run-one` runs this as a second bounded subprocess after the entrypoint
exits, with KLEIN_ARTIFACT pointing at the artifact the search produced, and
decides the run on the number THIS script prints. It is outside
study.yaml:entrypoint.mutable and is hashed at the METHOD gate: once E0001 has
run, a change here is refused.

Two design rules, and they are the whole point of the study:

1. **It shares no code with the search.** `lib/nothree.py` is never imported
   here and nothing here is importable from there. The search decides whether a
   candidate point is addable in O(k) by hashing normalized directions; this
   file re-derives the objective by enumerating every one of the C(k,3) triples
   and applying the integer cross-product test to each. The clever algorithm
   and the obvious one agreeing is evidence; one algorithm agreeing with itself
   would be a tautology.

2. **It is the dumbest correct thing.** No sampling, no early exit that could
   hide a triple, no floating point anywhere: `(b-a) x (c-a)` is an integer and
   the test is whether that integer is zero. C(62,3) = 37820 triples at the
   largest grid this study uses — a checker has no reason to be clever.

What it rejects, with exit code 2: a malformed artifact, a coordinate that is
not an integer, a point off the n x n grid, a repeated point, and any three
points on a common line. What it reports rather than rejects: `claim_excess`,
the searcher's own claimed objective minus the objective computed here. A
non-zero `claim_excess` is not the checker's business to punish — it is the
notary's, and it becomes a `verifier_disagreement` crash.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import json
import os
import sys
import time
from itertools import combinations

from kleinlib.eval import evaluate_scalar


class Rejected(Exception):
    """The artifact is not a valid no-three-in-line configuration."""


def _load(artifact_path: str) -> dict:
    try:
        with open(artifact_path, encoding="utf-8") as handle:
            payload = json.load(handle)
    except (OSError, ValueError) as exc:
        raise Rejected(f"artifact is not readable JSON: {exc}") from exc
    if not isinstance(payload, dict):
        raise Rejected("artifact must be a JSON object")
    return payload


def _points(payload: dict, n: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    raw = payload.get("points")
    if not isinstance(raw, list):
        raise Rejected("artifact has no `points` list")
    points: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    for index, item in enumerate(raw):
        if not isinstance(item, list) or len(item) != 2:
            raise Rejected(f"point {index} is not a [x, y] pair: {item!r}")
        x, y = item
        # bool is an int in Python; a lattice point is not True.
        for name, value in (("x", x), ("y", y)):
            if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int):
                raise Rejected(
                    f"point {index} has a non-integer {name} coordinate {value!r} — "
                    "a lattice point that is not one"
                )
        if not (0 <= x < n and 0 <= y < n):
            raise Rejected(f"point {index} = ({x}, {y}) is off the {n} x {n} grid")
        points.append((int(x), int(y)))
    if len(set(points)) != len(points):
        raise Rejected("the point list repeats a lattice point; a configuration is a set")
    return points


def check(artifact_path: str) -> tuple[float, dict[str, float]]:
    """Independently score the artifact; raise :class:`Rejected` on an invalid one.

    The controls live in `data/prepared/instances.json`, frozen at the DATA gate:
    a negative control (the Erdos parabola set, which this checker must ACCEPT
    and score at exactly 11) and twelve positive controls (planted invalid
    objects, on which this checker must FIRE). E0001 runs both batteries.
    """
    payload = _load(artifact_path)
    if payload.get("problem") != "no-three-in-line":
        raise Rejected(f"artifact declares problem {payload.get('problem')!r}")
    n = payload.get("n")
    if isinstance(n, bool) or not isinstance(n, int) or n < 1:
        raise Rejected(f"artifact declares a non-positive-integer grid size n={n!r}")
    points = _points(payload, n)

    triples = 0
    for a, b, c in combinations(points, 3):
        triples += 1
        cross = (b[0] - a[0]) * (c[1] - a[1]) - (b[1] - a[1]) * (c[0] - a[0])
        if cross == 0:
            raise Rejected(f"points {a}, {b} and {c} are collinear")

    claimed = payload.get("claimed_objective")
    if isinstance(claimed, bool) or not isinstance(claimed, int):
        raise Rejected(
            "artifact does not state an integer `claimed_objective`; a searcher that "
            "will not say what it thinks it found cannot be audited"
        )
    objective = len(points)
    return float(objective), {
        "grid_n": float(n),
        "triples_checked": float(triples),
        "claim_excess": float(claimed - objective),
    }


def main() -> None:
    t0 = time.time()
    artifact_path = os.environ.get("KLEIN_ARTIFACT")
    if not artifact_path:
        raise RuntimeError(
            "verify.py is run by `klein run-one`, which sets KLEIN_ARTIFACT to the "
            "artifact the entrypoint declared."
        )
    try:
        value, extra = check(artifact_path)
    except Rejected as exc:
        print(f"REJECTED: {exc}")
        sys.exit(2)
    evaluate_scalar(
        value,
        exp_id=os.environ.get("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID", "VERIFY"),
        # study_dir is deliberately NOT passed: aux_metrics.tsv is idempotent per
        # experiment id, so a verifier writing under the run's id would erase the
        # search telemetry the run just recorded. The verifier's own cost is on
        # the manifest as `verifier.wall_seconds`, where it belongs.
        study_dir=None,
        t0=t0,
        metric_name="points",
        metric_goal="higher",
        extra=extra,
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Things worth copying:

  • It imports nothing from the search. lib/nothree.py is not in its import list and never will be; that is what makes the two implementations' agreement evidence instead of a tautology.
  • It refuses before it scores. Malformed JSON, a non-integer coordinate (bool checked explicitly — True is an int in Python), a point off the grid, a repeated point. Well-formedness is the checker's job too.
  • It reports the searcher's self-report rather than punishing it. claim_excess = claimed - verified is printed as a number. Policing that difference is the notary's job, at tolerance: 0, and that separation is why E0008 became a crash rather than a silently-corrected score.
  • It writes no aux rows (study_dir=None). The aux sidecar is idempotent per experiment id, so a verifier writing under the run's id would erase the search telemetry the run had just recorded. Its own cost lands on the manifest as verifier.wall_seconds, where it belongs.

The cost of being dumb: 26235 cross products on the largest object, 0.002046 seconds. The benefit of being dumb is the planted control named parabola_plus_one — twelve points containing exactly one bad triple — which a checker that sampled triples would very likely have passed.

§6.2 · Freeze the controls before the checker meets them — C7

The twelve planted objects and the one known-valid object are written by prepare.py into the artifact the DATA gate hashes. The gate record carries its sha256; the file is regenerable from committed code and from nothing else.

payload = {
    "instances": {"n_small": {...}, "n_large": {...}},
    "seed_blocks": {"development": DEVELOPMENT_SEED, "sealed": SEALED_SEED},
    "budgets": BUDGETS,
    "controls": {
        "negative": {...},          # the Erdos parabola set, expected_objective = p
        "positive": {"count": 12, "objects": planted_invalid(N_SMALL)},
    },
}

It costs nothing to arrange and it removes a whole class of self-deception: a positive control that can be softened after it fails is not a control. The same file carries the seed blocks, which is how a study with no rows still gets a sealed access — and why no literal seed appears in any script this study owns.

§6.3 · Give the guard a test it can fail — C8

The per-experiment mutable surface is search.py, and a candidate is a cell: which mode, which rung of the frozen ladder. E0008's diff was three constants.

-CELL = "unassigned"        # the cell this candidate measures
-MODE = "search"            # controls | search | overclaim

One point of inflation — the smallest lie available — and the notary refused the run at tolerance: 0. If you ship a disagreement guard, spend one run proving it fires; documentation that a mechanism exists is not evidence that it works.

Here is the entrypoint the notary actually executed, in full. Note that it computes no objective that enters the ledger: it writes an object, states what it thinks that object is worth, and prints the artifact path the verifier will be pointed at.

"""Search for the object the verifier will grade.

This is the per-experiment mutable surface declared in study.yaml:entrypoint.
ONE falsifiable idea per candidate; `klein run-one` commits it before it runs.

A candidate here is a CELL: which mode, and which rung of the frozen budget
ladder. Everything else — the grid size, the seed blocks, the budget values, the
control objects — comes from `data/prepared/instances.json`, which the DATA gate
hashed, and the fixed machinery lives in `lib/nothree.py`. Nothing in this file
computes the objective that enters the ledger: it writes an object, states what
it thinks that object is worth, and the declared verifier decides.

  MODE = "controls"  -> run the frozen control battery against verify.py and
                        hand the notary the known-valid object (E0001)
  MODE = "search"    -> run the iterated local search at BUDGET (E0002-E0007,
                        E0009-E0010)
  MODE = "overclaim" -> search, then report OVERCLAIM_BY more points than the
                        object actually has (E0008, on purpose)
"""

import json
import os
import sys

from pathlib import Path

sys.path.insert(0, str(Path(__file__).resolve().parent))

from lib.nothree import (  # noqa: E402
    Search,

# --- the cell -------------------------------------------------------------
# Every candidate names its cell here and sets the three knobs below. The base
# state committed between runs is `unassigned`, so that the surface a discard
# restores is never itself a cell: on this study EVERY run is a discard (the
# frontier is seeded at the proven maximum), so without a cell label the surface
# would return to the previous candidate's exact configuration and `run-one`
# would rightly refuse the next one as an unchanged re-execution.
CELL = "unassigned"        # the cell this candidate measures
MODE = "search"            # controls | search | overclaim
BUDGET = "small"           # small | medium | large — a rung of the frozen ladder
OVERCLAIM_BY = 0           # points to add to the SEARCH's self-report, not to the object
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

SMOKE = os.environ.get("KLEIN_SMOKE") == "1"
SEALED_DRYRUN = os.environ.get("KLEIN_SEALED_DRYRUN") == "1"
EXPERIMENT_ID = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID") or ("SMOKE" if SMOKE else None)
TRACK = os.environ.get("KLEIN_TRACK") or ("n_small" if SMOKE else None)


def seed_block(evaluation_kind: str) -> str:
    """Which frozen seed block this run may use — the analogue of a partition.

    `development` is the only block adaptive work may touch. `final_test`
    selects `sealed`, the block no development run has used, and that is the
    track's one sealed access. The sealed REHEARSAL
    (`klein run-one --final-test --dry-run`) must not touch it: like
    `kleinlib.data.load_partition`, it hands back the development block and
    prints `sealed_dryrun: 1` so a silent success cannot be mistaken for a pass.
    """
    if evaluation_kind not in {"development", "final_test"}:
        raise RuntimeError(f"invalid KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND={evaluation_kind!r}")
    if evaluation_kind == "final_test" and SEALED_DRYRUN:
        print("sealed_dryrun: 1")
        return "development"
    return "sealed" if evaluation_kind == "final_test" else "development"


def block_fingerprint(track: str, n: int, block: str, seed: int) -> str:
    """What this study prints where a tabular study prints its partition hash.

    `data.split.kind` is `none`, so the DATA gate could realize no partition and
    the notary has nothing to compare against — it says so and proceeds. The
    fingerprint is printed anyway, because it is what makes each manifest carry
    a machine-readable record of WHICH seed block the run drew from: the sealed
    runs' manifests carry a different one from every development run's.
    """
    payload = json.dumps(
        {"track": track, "n": n, "block": block, "seed": seed},
        sort_keys=True,
        separators=(",", ":"),
    )
    return hashlib.sha256(payload.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()


def controls(instances: dict, out_dir: Path) -> tuple[list[tuple[int, int]], int, dict]:
    """Run the frozen control battery against the DECLARED verifier.

    Positive control: every planted invalid object must be REJECTED (the
    detector must fire). Negative control: the known-valid Erdos parabola set is
    handed to the notary as this cell's own artifact, so the acceptance half is
    performed by the notary's own verifier invocation rather than by this file.
    """
    positive = instances["controls"]["positive"]["objects"]
    rejected = 0
    verdicts = []
    for obj in positive:
        path = write_planted(out_dir, obj)
        proc = run_verifier(path, study_dir=".", experiment_id=EXPERIMENT_ID or "CONTROL")
        fired = proc.returncode != 0
        rejected += fired
        verdicts.append((obj["name"], proc.returncode))
        print(f"control {obj['name']}: exit={proc.returncode} {'REJECTED' if fired else 'ACCEPTED'}")
    negative = instances["controls"]["negative"]
    points = [(int(x), int(y)) for x, y in negative["points"]]
    print(f"control {negative['name']}: handed to the notary as this cell's artifact")
    return points, negative["expected_objective"], {
        "planted": float(len(positive)),
        "rejected": float(rejected),
        "verdicts": verdicts,
    }


def main() -> None:
    t0 = time.time()
    evaluation_kind = os.environ.get("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND")
    if SMOKE:
        evaluation_kind = evaluation_kind or "development"
    missing = [
        name
        for name, value in (
            ("KLEIN_EVALUATION_KIND", evaluation_kind),
            ("KLEIN_EXPERIMENT_ID", EXPERIMENT_ID),
            ("KLEIN_TRACK", TRACK),
        )
        if value is None
    ]
    if missing:
        raise RuntimeError(
            "search.py must be invoked through `klein run-one`. For a pre-run "
            "syntax/shape check use `KLEIN_SMOKE=1 python search.py` — it prints "
            "the canonical block, writes no sidecars or snapshots, and is not "
            "evidence. Missing: " + ", ".join(missing)
        )

    instances = load_instances(".")
    n = int(instances["instances"][TRACK]["n"])
    block = seed_block(evaluation_kind)
    seed = int(instances["seed_blocks"][block])
    budget = int(instances["budgets"][BUDGET])
    if SMOKE:
        budget = 200

    out_dir = Path("models") / ("_smoke" if SMOKE else EXPERIMENT_ID)
    out_dir.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)

    extra: dict[str, float] = {}
    search_t0 = time.perf_counter()
    if MODE == "controls":
        points, claimed, control_extra = controls(instances, out_dir)
        extra["planted"] = control_extra["planted"]
        extra["rejected"] = control_extra["rejected"]
        extra["evaluations"] = 0.0
        extra["passes"] = 0.0
        extra["best_at_evaluation"] = 0.0
    else:
        result = Search(n=n, seed=seed, budget=budget).run()
        points = result.points
        claimed = result.objective + (OVERCLAIM_BY if MODE == "overclaim" else 0)
        extra["evaluations"] = float(result.evaluations)
        extra["passes"] = float(result.passes)
        extra["best_at_evaluation"] = float(result.best_at_evaluation)
    extra["search_seconds"] = time.perf_counter() - search_t0
    extra["grid_n"] = float(n)
    extra["budget"] = float(budget)
    extra["search_seed"] = float(seed)
    extra["sealed_block"] = 1.0 if block == "sealed" else 0.0
    extra["overclaim_by"] = float(OVERCLAIM_BY if MODE == "overclaim" else 0)
    extra["claimed_objective"] = float(claimed)
    extra["object_size"] = float(len(points))

    artifact = write_solution(
        out_dir / "solution.json",
        n=n,
        points=points,
        claimed_objective=claimed,
        meta={
            "mode": MODE,
            "track": TRACK,
            "seed_block": block,
            "budget_rung": BUDGET,
            "experiment": EXPERIMENT_ID,
        },
    )
    print(f"solution: {artifact.as_posix()}")

    evaluate_scalar(
        float(claimed),
        exp_id=EXPERIMENT_ID,
        study_dir=None if SMOKE else ".",
        t0=t0,
        metric_name="points",
        metric_goal="higher",
        split_fingerprint=block_fingerprint(TRACK, n, block, seed),
        extra=extra,
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

§6.5 · Three pitfalls this study actually hit

  1. Every run restores the surface, so the base state must not be a cell. With zero keeps by construction, run-one restores search.py after every run — to the previous candidate's exact configuration, which it would then refuse as an unchanged re-execution. The fix is a CELL label whose base value ("unassigned") is never a cell any run uses.
  2. An adjective is not a numeral. "Sub-millisecond" travelled from a pre-run estimate on the data card into a locked claim, past every mechanical check, because no scan looks at words. Re-read your qualitative claims against the logs before you lock them. (§5.5; erratum E1.)
  3. The budget unit can hide the thing you are comparing. Counting addability tests is honest about cost and silent about effort: the same 2000000 tests bought 19014 greedy completions at n = 11 and 2190 at n = 31. If two instances are to be compared, register the unit that makes them comparable.

7 · Next steps & references

§7.1 · What to try next

  1. Register a seed sweep at n = 11 and the largest budget. The cheapest and the most important. E0004 and E0009 differ only in the seed and land on opposite sides of the proven maximum, so the quantity this study could not measure is the success rate — and a rate is exactly what a registered measurement sweep is for (klein sweep register, every trial to a sidecar). Thirty seeds at about a second each would turn C5 from an observation into a number.
  2. Locate where the reach breaks. The two instances are far apart: the maximum is reachable at one, out of reach at the other, and everything between is unmeasured. A third instance around n = 19 — the size at which integer programming is still provably optimal (ref:ramanathan2025) — would say whether the fall-off is a cliff or a slope. It needs a third track declared at CONSULT, which is exactly why this study did not add one after seeing its results.
  3. Budget in passes, not evaluations. Register the ladder in greedy completions so the two instances are compared at equal search effort rather than equal cost, and the pair of ladders side by side separates "the instance is harder" from "the budget bought less".
  4. Change the perturbation. The current move removes one to three random points; a move that removes a whole row's pair respects the structure a configuration must have. That is a change to lib/, which this study may not make mid-loop, so it belongs to a follow-up whose predictions cite 11-exact-verifier-construction#C1 and #C5.
