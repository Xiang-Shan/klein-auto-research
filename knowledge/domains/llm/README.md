# LLM evaluation

Seeded 2026-11-03 by study 20 (`20-llm-cot-contamination`: chain-of-thought versus a number-only prompt on GSM8K and on procedurally generated template twins, one hosted model snapshot, temperature 0). Doctrine anchor: the item is the unit of uncertainty and two configurations are compared paired on the same items; every model call goes through the call ledger; re-querying is a replication and replaying the ledger is not; a public benchmark is presumed contaminated and the sealed comparison is a fresh block the model cannot have seen.

**Scope of every line below.** The seeding study measured one model, `qwen-flash-2025-07-28`, with thinking off and two registered prompt wordings. The confirmed lines are statements about that snapshot and those prompts. What travels is the procedure, not the effect sizes.

## Typed citations

- **"Chain-of-thought gains on GSM8K are large" needs a qualifier: large for models whose direct answers are terse.** On 300 public GSM8K items, chain-of-thought beat the registered number-only prompt by +0.01 exact-match, paired item-bootstrap interval [-0.01, 0.03]. On fresh template twins the gain was -0.025 on the development block and 0.000 on the sealed block. Every interval excludes the registered gain of 0.10. This is a confirmed null for one snapshot. It does not contradict the papers that reported large gains, because their models answered tersely when asked to, and this one did not (next line). `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C2)` `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C4)`
- **Audit the direct arm's compliance from the ledger before reading any CoT-versus-direct gain.** Only 4 of 300 public replies to "answer with the final number only" were a bare number. The direct arm's median output was 112.5 tokens against 243 for chain-of-thought, and chain-of-thought wrote only 1.85 times the direct arm's tokens. The comparison had become chain-of-thought against a terse chain-of-thought. A scorer that falls back to the last number in a reply still scores such an arm, so the arm silently turns into a different arm. Count bare replies and the token ratio per arm before computing the gain. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C5)` `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C9)`
- **A gain cannot exceed one minus the baseline score, so measure the baseline before registering the threshold.** At a direct score of 0.95, a registered gain of at least 0.10 could not be reached on the public items. When the threshold was registered the direct score was unknown, so the refutation could not have been foreseen. The lesson is to pilot the baseline, or to choose items off the ceiling, first. The study's reading that the ceiling, rather than the model, removed the gain is a hypothesis: the gain was measured with a tight interval, and whether a compliant direct arm would open headroom is untested. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C10)` `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C6)` `(see 20-llm-cot-contamination erratum E1)`
- **On twin blocks, the inference is a cluster bootstrap over templates, not McNemar's test.** The four twins of one template share wording and structure. McNemar's p of 0.125 on the development twins treats them as independent and overstates the evidence. On independent public items McNemar is valid. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C14)`
- **Size a template-paired contamination probe to the gap it must resolve, and register the rule on the interval, not the point estimate.** With 50 templates the interval on the original-minus-twin gap was 0.075 wide on the development twins and 0.105 wide on the sealed twins. A gap of 0.05 could be neither detected nor excluded. Plan on roughly four times the templates to halve the interval. The study's point-estimate rules "gap at most 0.05" were supported at 0.02 and 0.01 while the intervals' upper ends were 0.06 and 0.065, so "supported" there means the point estimate is below the bound, not the gap. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C11)` `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C3)`
- **Temperature 0 on a hosted model is not determinism, and a gap this small can change sign under re-query.** A salted re-query of the anchor cell changed 9 of 600 item-arm scores. A re-query of the development contamination cell moved the gap from 0.02 to -0.025, a shift of 0.045 inside the pre-registered tolerance of 0.05. The study reads this as serving noise setting the sign of any gap below about 0.05 on 50 originals. That reading is an exploratory interpretation. The flips themselves are measured. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C13)` `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C7)`
- **A ledger replay audits the scorer; only a re-query says anything about the model.** The offline replay of the anchor cell reproduced with a difference of 0 because it re-scored the same recorded answers. The salted re-query of the same cell moved the gain by 0.0167. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C13)`
- **Cells that share ledger entries are one measurement.** The sealed contamination cell made no new model call. It re-scored the 50 original and 200 sealed chain-of-thought answers that two earlier cells had already recorded, so its agreement with them holds by construction. De-duplication keeps a study cheap and replayable. Findings have to say which cells it couples. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C12)`
- **At the ceiling, a positive control can only plant what the ceiling leaves room for.** A planted gain of 0.10 was recovered in all three gain cells, with an interval wholly above zero on the public items ([0.0733, 0.15]). A single seeded 0.05 plant was detected on the public items and on neither twin block. Each plant is one seeded realization, not a power estimate. The interval widths above are the better basis for a detection limit. `(supports 20-llm-cot-contamination#C17)` `(see 20-llm-cot-contamination erratum E2)`

## Open questions this domain inherits

- Does an enforced bare-number arm (a few-token `max_tokens` cap, or a response-format constraint, with compliance registered as its own prediction) reopen the chain-of-thought gain on the same items? This is the cheapest test of the ceiling hypothesis above, and it is item 1 of the seeding study's `findings.md` §⑦.
- What is the gain on items where the direct arm scores well below 0.95, so that a registered gain of 0.10 is reachable?
- What is a hosted model's re-query flip rate, measured as `fit_noise` from at least three salted re-queries of one cell? The seeding study's tolerances were pre-registered replication tolerances, not measured floors.
