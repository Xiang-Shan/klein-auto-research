# Mathematics

Seeded by study 11 (exact-verifier construction); extended 2026-11-03 by study 24 (Golomb rulers: an LLM proposer against classical search under one exact verifier). Doctrine anchor: the verifier is the only judge; a found construction is a lower bound and nothing more; a search failure is never evidence of impossibility; "proved" needs a pinned proof artifact.

## Typed citations

- **The checker should be the dumbest correct implementation, and it must share no code with the search.** Two independent implementations of the same predicate agreeing is evidence; one implementation agreeing with itself is a tautology. In the seeding study the search decided collinearity in O(k) by hashing normalized directions while the checker enumerated every triple with an integer cross product, at a cost of 26235 tests on the largest object it verified — 0.002046 seconds there, and 0.000169 seconds on a 22-point object — and it caught a planted object whose twelve points contained exactly one bad triple, which a checker that sampled triples would have passed. An exhaustive checker is cheap, not free: say what its log says, not what a pre-run estimate said. `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C6)` `(see 11-exact-verifier-construction erratum E1)`
- **Freeze a verifier's controls before the verifier meets them, and give the disagreement guard a test it can fail.** The seeding study hashed twelve planted invalid objects and one known-valid object into its DATA gate record before any run existed, then spent one of ten runs making the searcher overclaim by a single point: the notary recorded `reported 21.0` against `verified 20.0` as a crash with an `NA` ledger row, so the inflated number reached no table. A control that can be softened after it fails is not a control, and a guard that has never fired is a guard nobody has tested. `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C7)` `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C8)`
- **A heuristic search that misses a known-attainable value has told you about that run, not about the problem — and often not even about the search.** In the seeding study the same iterated local search, on the same instance at the same budget, scored 21 from one seed block and 22 — the proven maximum — from another. Report "did not reach it under budget B", never "cannot"; and treat one run's outcome as a draw from a distribution until a registered seed sweep turns it into a rate. `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C5)` `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C4)`
- **When the best known value for a problem equals a proven bound, the frontier is closed before the first run — do that arithmetic first.** The seeding study's two tracks both had `metric.bound.ideal` equal to `metric.incumbent_external.value` (22 at n = 11, 62 at n = 31), so headroom was exactly zero and no run could ever be a keep. It acknowledged the closed door with `klein headroom ack` and redefined success as attaining the known maximum rather than beating it, before spending anything. `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C9)`

### LLM proposals against classical search (study 24)

Scope: Golomb rulers, one exact verifier shared by two registered arms, scored against the tabulated minimum lengths of OEIS A003022. One arm was a randomized construction plus local search; the other was one model snapshot, `qwen-flash-2025-07-28` at a single temperature, emitting rulers as JSON data, never code. Each arm had 2000 candidate evaluations per (order, trial) pair: development orders n = 8 to 11 at three trials each (12 pairs per arm), sealed orders n = 12 and 13 (6 pairs per arm). Every order's answer is public, so "matched" means the best verified ruler has the tabulated length and nothing more. The study ran no contamination control and no no-feedback arm, and it says so.

- **A count difference on a few pairs is a measurement, not a rate — and it can vanish on the next orders.** On development the LLM arm matched the tabulated length in 6 of 12 pairs and the search arm in none, a difference of +6 pairs. The exact McNemar p was 0.03125 with pairs as the unit, which does not clear the Bonferroni level of 0.025 for the study's two arm contrasts. On the sealed orders both arms matched 0 of 6, and the LLM arm produced no verified ruler at all: an invalid best in 6 of 6 pairs, 265 of 1200 replies unparsed, and none of its candidates a valid Golomb ruler. Report a development advantage as "matched on these orders under budget B", and expect a sealed lane on harder orders to test whether it carries. Here it did not. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C3)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C2)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C5)`
- **A fixed evaluation budget shows hardness growing with order; read a classical arm's misses against its own budget curve.** At 2000 evaluations per pair the search arm matched in 0 of 12 development pairs, its mean gap to the tabulated length growing from 5.667 to 35.333 length units across orders 8 to 11, and a re-execution reproduced the count exactly. On the sealed orders its best verified lengths stayed at least 48 length units above the table. That describes this annealer at this budget, not classical search in general: no published heuristic was run. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C1)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C5)`
- **Match the verifier budget, and report the compute beside it — they are different budgets.** Matching candidate evaluations (verifier calls) is the fair comparison of what each arm was allowed to ask the judge. It is not a match in wall-clock, tokens or information. In the study the LLM arm took about 10234 times the search arm's searcher-reported wall-clock on development and 13016 times on the sealed orders, and the study made 6000 live model calls carrying 6507285 tokens. A claim that one arm is "more efficient" must name which budget it means. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C10)`
- **A match on a tabulated instance cannot separate search from recall.** A matched-budget comparison on instances whose answers are public measures recall and search together. A claim about search needs a contamination control (an untabulated or perturbed instance whose optimum the verifier can certify) and a no-feedback arm before it is made. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C15)`
- **The recall reading of the study's matches is an untested hypothesis, and the evidence for it is weak.** Every matched ruler is the row the public table prints, and the best ruler in all three trials at order ten is the first ten marks of the tabulated eleven-mark ruler — a Golomb ruler by inheritance, at length 70 against the tabulated 55. The locked sentence called the optimum unique; it is unique only up to reflection at n = 8, and n = 11 has two optima up to reflection. Across every proposal, the mirror rows and the second eleven-mark optimum were proposed 0 times, so identity carries weak, post-hoc information leaning toward retrieval of the printed rows. It is still not a test. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C7)` `(see 24-golomb-llm-vs-search erratum E1)`
- **"Recall shows up in the first batch" is a testable signature, and here it failed — which does not exclude recall.** Only 1 of 12 development pairs held a verified optimum in its first proposal batch, and 2 of 12 in a salted re-query; the matches arrived after verifier feedback, as late as proposal 74. The method card's prior that first-batch matches would be at least half the matched count (a bar of 3) was refuted at 1. A late match can be a resampled or repaired memory, so the absence of the signature leaves both readings open; only a no-feedback arm separates resampling from feedback-steered repair. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C4)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C9)`
- **Count an LLM proposer's outcomes by order, not only by pair.** The trials of an order sent byte-identical first requests, and every development order came out all-or-none for the LLM arm. Moving the unit from pair to order moved the exact test from 0.03125 to 0.5. Trials that share a prompt are not independent draws; register the order (or the prompt) as the unit before the comparison. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C11)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C3)`
- **A miss is a fact about the proposer at its budget, never evidence that a match is out of reach.** The matched rulers re-attain upper bounds already known to be tight — length 34 at eight marks and 72 at eleven, per the OEIS table — so the study established nothing new about any Golomb length. The LLM arm's failure to write a single valid 12- or 13-mark ruler describes this snapshot under this prompt, and the search arm's sealed gaps describe this annealer at this budget. This is study 11's lesson again, now with an LLM proposer. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C16)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C5)` `(supports 11-exact-verifier-construction#C5)`

## Open questions this domain inherits

- How often does a budgeted iterated local search reach 2n on the no-three-in-line problem at a given n? The seeding study measured a difference between two seeds and explicitly declined to call it a rate; a registered seed sweep is the first item in its `findings.md` §⑦.
- Where does a general heuristic's reach break as n grows? The seeding study measured n = 11 and n = 31 and nothing between.
- Are an LLM proposer's matches on tabulated combinatorial objects search or retrieval? Study 24 could not say. Its first next step is the same proposer, unchanged, on a perturbed variant whose optima the verifier can certify by enumeration at small n; a match there could not be retrieval. A no-feedback arm (the same prompt resampled without verdicts) would then say whether late matches are resampling or feedback-steered repair. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C15)` `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C4)`
- Why did the LLM arm produce no valid ruler at order nine while matching at order eleven? The study's hypothesis, untested, is that its proposals anchor on extending the tabulated eight-mark row, and extending a Golomb ruler by a mark usually repeats a difference; it saw no valid ruler in 600 order-nine proposals. `(supports 24-golomb-llm-vs-search#C8)`
