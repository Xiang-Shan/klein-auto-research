# ML research

Seeded by study 13 (fixed-budget character language model). Doctrine anchor: compare at matched compute in steps or tokens; report seed variance as fit noise and never paste it as a bar; the training script never grades itself — a verifier scores the checkpoint.

## Typed citations

- **At a fixed STEP budget the binding constraint is optimization progress per step, so the schedule pays and the capacity does not.** In the seeding study six single changes to a 4-layer character transformer were measured at exactly 2000 optimizer steps each, against a floor of 0.0149525 nats. The only improvement was a pure learning-rate schedule change worth 0.052855 nats, while multiplying the parameter count by 3.908075 moved the loss -0.4742 floors — inside the floor and in the wrong direction. This is not a contradiction of the scaling literature: that work grows the model at fixed FLOPs and grows tokens alongside it, and a fixed step budget does neither, so the wider model never gets the extra passes it would need. Reach for the schedule before the width whenever the budget is counted in steps. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C9)` `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C1)`
- **A technique transfers only with its mechanism, and weight tying is the counter-example.** Tying the output head to the token embedding is treated as a free parameter saving. At a 65-character vocabulary the seeding study measured it at -11.3693 floors — the largest single effect it found anywhere, and a cost — because one 65-row matrix was forced to serve as both the input lookup and the output classifier with no logit scale between the two very different norms they want. The parameter arithmetic that predicted the change would be invisible (8320 of 824320 parameters, 1.009317 per cent) was the wrong quantity to reason about. Before importing a result, check that the mechanism it rests on is present at your scale. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C6)` `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C1)`
- **An ablation against the wrong partner answers a different question than the one you asked.** The seeding study measured a 200-step warmup at -1.0431 floors against a CONSTANT learning rate, and the same warmup at 0.103528 floors — inside the floor — once it was paired with the decay warmup is normally used with. Both numbers are correct and only the second is about warmup. When a lever is conventionally used with a partner, either test it with the partner or say plainly which pairing was measured. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C1)`
- **Measure the floor on the difference, not on the level.** Five identically-configured runs of the seeding study's anchor recipe had a level spread of standard deviation 0.00802952 nats; the ten paired differences between those same runs, re-scored on identical validation windows, had standard deviation 0.00747623 and range 0.026494, which is what set the bar of 0.0149525 nats. A candidate-versus-anchor comparison produces a difference, so the spread of differences is the quantity it has to clear — and a seed-only level spread is provenance about the fit, never the bar. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C8)`
- **The training script must not grade its own checkpoint, and the cost of fixing that is one file.** In the seeding study a separate verifier — its own implementation of the architecture, its own window enumeration, its own loss, run by the notary in a fresh process on the artifact from disk — decided every disposition, read the optimizer-step count out of the checkpoint so that matched compute was enforced rather than promised, and never disagreed with the training script by more than 0.0 nats against a declared tolerance of 0.01. Two implementations agreeing is evidence; one implementation agreeing with itself is not. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C10)` `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C5)`
- **A language-model harness needs a copy-the-input control.** The classic bug is aligning targets with inputs instead of shifting them by one, and it makes a model look extraordinary rather than broken. A predictor that bets the next character equals the current one scores 4.750043 nats under a correct harness — worse than chance at 4.174388 — and would score 0.693147 under the bug. In the seeding study no other control could have caught it: chance, an untrained network and a unigram all behave identically either way. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C11)`
- **A contiguous-block split of ordered text buys leakage safety and pays for it in distribution shift; measure both.** The seeding study's cross-partition audit found nothing to memorise (largest 8-gram Jaccard similarity 0.030059), and its sealed final tenth still came back 0.046961 nats — 3.140679 measured floors — harder than development for the same recipe trained on more data. That is a property of the corpus, not of the model. A shuffled split would have hidden the shift and created the leak instead. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C12)` `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C7)`

## Open questions this domain inherits

- Where is the exchange rate between width and steps? The seeding study measured width at matched STEPS and found nothing; the matched-FLOPs version (a wider model with proportionally fewer steps) is the first item in its `findings.md` §⑦ and would turn "capacity does not pay at fixed steps" into a statement about where the trade actually sits.
- Is the weight-tying collapse about the shared norm or about the parameter sharing? A single learned logit scale on the tied head separates the two explanations in one run, and the seeding study did not spend it.
- How much of the seeding study's run-to-run spread is the seed and how much is backend nondeterminism? A full re-execution at the SAME seed on the same accelerator moved 0.000962 nats, 0.119808 fit-noise standard deviations, while re-scoring a saved checkpoint moved nothing at all — so some of what a seed sweep attributes to the seed is the backend. `(supports 13-charlm-fixed-budget#C2)`
